# Professional cryptanalysis of the Voynich manuscript and the cipher/encoding hypotheses (1910s to October 2026)

Notes compiled 2026-10-17 14:59 PDT (date from system clock). Sources were web pages fetched this session. Three primary PDFs could not be fetched (HTTP 403): the NSA copies of Tiltman's 1967 paper and D'Imperio's 1978 monograph, and the Cryptologia full text of Greshko 2025. Claims about those works rest on secondary summaries (voynich.nu, ciphermysteries.com, Wikipedia, news coverage, forum threads), and each is marked where that matters.

## Early "solutions" and how they collapsed (Newbold, Feely, Strong, Brumbaugh)

### Takeaway
Every pre-1980 claimed decipherment used a method flexible enough to produce almost any desired plaintext: anagramming, subjective abbreviation-expansion, or undisclosed multi-step keys. None explained the text's highly repetitive, rigid structure. Each was rejected once someone tested whether the method was unique and reproducible.

### Cited Findings
- **Newbold (1919–1928).** William Romaine Newbold was a University of Pennsylvania philosophy professor. He read the irregular edges of strokes under magnification, not the letters themselves, then applied a complicated anagram scheme over 55–110 characters. His plaintext had Roger Bacon using a telescope and microscope, and he "read" a spiral nebula on one page. He presented in April 1921, and his book appeared posthumously in 1928, edited by Roland Grubb Kent. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html)
- **Manly's refutation (1931).** John Manly published after Newbold and Voynich had died. He judged the unreliability of anagram-based solutions the main flaw. Wikipedia's summary adds that the "shorthand" markings were cracks in drying ink and that the method could yield almost any reading. Manly also left a statement, whose meaning is unclear, that the manuscript is a relatively simple substitution cipher with many nulls. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- **Feely (1943).** Joseph Martin Feely was a Rochester, NY lawyer. He proposed a simple substitution of heavily abbreviated Latin (a Bacon scientific diary). He never saw copies of the manuscript and worked only from the illustrations in Newbold/Kent. No one accepted it. Tiltman wrote that "His unmethodical method produced text in unacceptable medieval Latin, in unauthentic abbreviated forms." — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html). D'Imperio (1978) is cited as saying other scholars "unanimously rejected" it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- **Strong (1945/1947).** Leonell C. Strong was a Yale medical scientist (cancer researcher) who worked from a few pages in Newbold's book. He built a complicated polyalphabetic ("multiple-alphabet arithmetic") cipher whose details he never disclosed and which can only be partly reconstructed. It "revealed" the author as Anthony Askham/Ascham. Objections: one transliteration error changes everything, the method does not explain the text's repetitiveness, and word matches occur by chance. Notes released after his death show his final word-selection stage was subjective. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript). The date is reported as both 1945 (the brief's framing) and 1947 (Wikipedia). I did not resolve this.
- **Brumbaugh (1970s).** Robert S. Brumbaugh was a Yale professor of medieval philosophy. He took the manuscript to be a Dee/Kelley fraud on Rudolf II, but still assumed an underlying text. His method mapped Roman letters to digits 0–9 and the digits back onto the manuscript's alphabet, so it was many-to-one and ambiguous. He published plant and zodiac label "solutions" in 1974 and 1976, and edited a 1978 anthology. D'Imperio was supportive, but voynich.nu calls the plaintext "far from convincing." — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html)
- **Radiocarbon dating (2009, University of Arizona, Greg Hodgins).** Four vellum samples dated to 1404–1438 at 95% confidence. This rules out Roger Bacon, retroactively undercutting Newbold, Feely and Strong's Bacon/Askham framings, and it makes Dee/Kelley-era fraud (Brumbaugh, Rugg) unlikely unless the forger used old vellum. — [LiveScience](https://livescience.com/12838-mystery-book-secret-110211-html.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript) (secondary sources; the original lab report was not checked)

### Inferences
- The common failure mode is under-determination. The analyst's free choices (anagram order, which abbreviation, which homophone) carry more information than the ciphertext does, so the "plaintext" reflects the analyst. Manly's critique of Newbold is the template professionals later applied to almost all claims.

- I did not read Manly's 1931 Speculum article directly.
- I could not resolve the exact date of Strong's publication (1945 vs 1947).

## The Friedman groups, Tiltman, Currier and D'Imperio: what the professionals concluded

### Takeaway
The NSA-lineage analysts converged on a negative result: the text is not a simple encipherment of a natural language in natural letter order. Friedman concluded it was an a priori constructed language. Tiltman found word structure built from fixed beginnings, middles and endings and called it a "cumbersome mixture of different types of substitution," doubting Friedman's language theory. Currier found two statistical "languages" tied to hands and showed that the line is a functional unit, concluding "there has to be an intermediate step somewhere." D'Imperio's 1978 monograph synthesized all of this into the standard reference for about 30 years, without a solution.

### Cited Findings
- **First Study Group (1944–46).** It was led by William Friedman with wartime specialists (Caldwell, McCracken, Miller, Puckett, Wilbur, Rhoads, Seaman). It devised a transliteration alphabet and transliterated most of the manuscript, put lines on IBM punch cards, and worked on a "letter-based cipher" hypothesis. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- **Second Study Group (1962–63).** The group did not call itself this. Jim Reeds researched it, and an audio tape of its opening session survives. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html). It reportedly produced a 692-page "Keyword in Context" cross-reference held in the Friedman Collection at the George C. Marshall Foundation Library (item 1609.3, not online). Reeds found the RCA transcription printout close to illegible. — [Voynich Ninja thread](https://www.voynich.ninja/printthread.php?tid=4036) (forum; unverified). Ciphermysteries confirms the 1944–46 and 1962–63 dates. — [ciphermysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/?p=28)
- **Elizebeth Friedman (1962)** described statistical analyses as "doomed to utter frustration." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- **Friedman's synthetic-language view.** Friedman proposed that the manuscript is text in a synthetic language built on strict logical principles. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html). He hid this as an anagram in a footnote to a Chaucer paper he co-wrote in Philological Quarterly, and the editor opened the solution after his death. It reads: "The Voynich Manuscript was an early attempt to construct an artificial or universal language of the a priori type.—Friedman." — [Tablet Magazine](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/arts-letters/articles/cracking-the-voynich-code). An ACM column summarizes his final view as a language "invented, de novo," and not a cipher. — [CACM search snippet](https://cacm.acm.org/opinion/there-is-nothing-new-under-the-sun) (page returned 403; snippet only). Friedman told Tiltman that the manuscript resembled Cave Beck's 1657 universal language. — [ciphermysteries Tiltman tag](https://ciphermysteries.com/tag/john-tiltman)
- **Tiltman.** The paper is "The Voynich Manuscript: 'The Most Mysterious Manuscript in the World'," dated 3/4/1967 in the NSA index and published in the NSA Technical Journal, Summer 1967. NSA released the current version in 2002. — [NSA index](https://media.defense.gov/2021/Jul/13/2002761423/-1/-1/0/NSA-INDEX-FOR-ATTEMPTS-TO-DECIPHER-THE-VOYNICH-MANUSCRIPT.PDF); [U Toronto catalog](https://omeka.ischool.utoronto.ca/items/show/126). Friedman sent him the final (recipes) quire, and his analysis dates from about 1950 (Wikipedia). He concluded that the text cannot result from applying a standard cipher to plaintext. He discussed a universal language without clearly confirming or rejecting it. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- **Tiltman's word-structure table (Plate 17, as summarized by Pelling in EVA terms).**
  - Roots: ok/of, ot/op, qok/qof, ch/sh, s, d, and lk/lf.
  - Suffixes: e/ee/eee + y or dy, or one or more groups from the a-family (an, ain, aiin, aiiin, ar, air, al) plus or/ol.
  - Order: "ar ar al" is a common triple, and al often follows ar but rarely precedes it.
  - Beginnings: paragraphs open with gallows k/f or t/p; line-initial y is common.
  - Middles: ckh/cth are infixes inside ch.
  - Endings: m occurs mostly at line end.
  - Quotes: the text "cannot be the result of substituting single symbols for letters in the natural order"; "My analysis revealed to me a cumbersome mixture of different types of substitution." Pelling says Tiltman "was plainly far from convinced" by the universal-language idea. — [ciphermysteries Tiltman tag](https://ciphermysteries.com/tag/john-tiltman). Ciphermysteries calls this a 1968 paper, a minor date conflict with the 1967 NSA index.
- **Currier (seminar, 30 Nov 1976, moderated by D'Imperio).**
  - Languages: "Languages" A and B are statistical regimes, not necessarily different languages. They could be dialects, subject matter or different encipherments. — [Goodreads blog summary](https://goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24890557-voynich-reconsidered-scribes-and-languages); [voynich.nu Currier](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/curr_main.html)
  - Hands: hands 1 and 2 correlate perfectly with A and B. Overall he saw two languages and "six to eight scribes." The biological section is all B in one hand.
  - Line as unit: line-initial and line-final symbol frequencies differ markedly from line-internal ones. Some characters rarely or never begin a line. One symbol occurs at line end 85% of the time. In about 25,000 words of herbal and biological text, no repeated sequence crosses a line break. He concluded the line is a functional entity of unknown function: "I can't interpret the data!"
  - Cipher verdict: the text is not "a straightforward simple encipherment of any linguistic data"; "there has to be an intermediate step somewhere." On the units: "they're _not words!_" He argued against doodling because at least six people were involved. — [voynich.nu Currier paper](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/curr_main.html)
  - Later refinements: Lisa Fagin Davis's palaeographic hand identification (she counts five scribes) superseded Currier's hands. René Zandbergen's 2024 classification adds a "language C" between A and B. — [search summary citing voynich.nu rz_lang](https://voynich.nu/extra/rz_lang.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- **D'Imperio, *The Voynich Manuscript: An Elegant Enigma* (NSA, 1978).** Tiltman introduced her to the manuscript in 1975, and she organized the 1976 seminar. The monograph summarized all recorded work to that date and was the standard reference for about thirty years. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html). Her separate HMM/cluster-analysis work concluded that the plaintext is probably not a natural language written with a single-letter alphabet. It found the system is probably not any form of single substitution, and probably not a natural language in impressionistic spelling. — [voynich.nu a2_char](https://voynich.nu/a2_char.html); NSA "An Application of Cluster Analysis" ([NSA PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2021/Jul/13/2002761422/-1/-1/0/AN-APPLICATION-OF-CLUSTER-ANALYSIS.PDF), not opened)

### Inferences
- The professional consensus by 1978 was not "unsolvable." It was narrower: not a one-step cipher over a European plaintext in natural order. This left open (a) multi-step or verbose systems ("intermediate step"), (b) constructed languages, (c) unfamiliar natural languages, and (d) meaningless text.
- Currier's line effects and Tiltman's slot structure are the two observations that every later cipher model must reproduce. Naibbe (2025) reproduces the second but explicitly not the first (see below).

## Cipher families argued against, and on what evidence

### Takeaway
- Simple substitution of a European language: effectively ruled out by entropy. Substitution leaves conditional entropy unchanged, and Voynichese h2 (about 2.2) is far below Latin, Italian and German (about 3.0–3.4).
- Standard polyalphabetic ciphers: argued against because they flatten character frequencies and raise entropy, while Voynichese is low-entropy and rigidly structured.
- Large nomenclators and codebooks: disfavoured by the small glyph inventory, but not strictly excluded.
- Classical transposition: hard to square with stable word-internal slot structure.
- Verbose ciphers, possibly homophonic, remain the most viable cipher family. Naibbe (2025) is the best existence proof, with acknowledged failures.

### Cited Findings
- **Entropy evidence.**
  - Bennett's figures for the Voynich text: h1 3.66 (European languages 3.91–4.14), h2 2.22 (3.01–3.37), h3 1.86 (2.12–2.62).
  - Corroboration: Stallings confirmed the figures, and Zandbergen found no language that matches.
  - Partial matches: Hawaiian matches the low values but fails on other grounds; Tagalog and Minjiang are nearby.
  - Lindemann & Bowern: conditional entropy is lower than in any of 316 comparison texts. They attribute this mainly to common characters being restricted to word positions.
  - Effect on simple substitution: a consistent symbol replacement does not change entropy, so "translating the text into Greek, Latin, English, or similar languages by simple substitution must fail." Any European plaintext must have been transformed by a process that drastically changed its statistics.
  - Caveat: transliteration choices (how glyph sequences are composed into "characters") significantly affect entropy values. — [voynich.nu a2_char](https://voynich.nu/a2_char.html); [Lindemann & Bowern, lingbuzz](https://lingbuzz.net/lingbuzz/005522/v1.pdf)
- **Per-family summary from Wikipedia.**
  - Substitution: excluded because the frequency profile does not resemble a known language.
  - Polyalphabetic (Alberti, 1460s; Vigenère): such ciphers yield roughly equal symbol probabilities, unlike the manuscript.
  - Nomenclator and homophonic: argued against because they typically need larger cipher alphabets than the 20–25 shapes used.
  - Verbose: tightly grouped shapes (or, ar, aiin) may be verbose units.
  - Repetition: near-identical words repeat unusually often, and the same word can appear up to five times in a row, so single-substitution decipherments yield babble.
  - Steganography: a Cardan grille is unlikely because the text is not on a regular grid. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript) (secondary)
- **Wider cipher classes.** A 2022–23 CEUR workshop paper (vol. 3313, paper 6) reportedly finds that "multiple types of encipherment which reduce conditional entropy exist," and that word-level predictability is not conclusive evidence of encipherment. — [CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/paper6.pdf) (only the search snippet; I could not extract the PDF text, so title and authors are unverified)
- **Informal entropy simulation (forum).** Among historical cipher types, only homophonic variants produced an entropy-profile "bump" like the manuscript's; the others dropped off too steeply. — [Voynich Ninja portal/thread](https://www.voynich.ninja/portal.php?page=47) (forum; self-reported; the poster concedes their homophonic cipher is not historically authentic)
- **Pelling's verbose-cipher hypothesis.** Common glyph pairs and groups act as single plaintext letters: qo, ol, al, or, ar, am, and the "4o" (EVA qo) token. The expansion is partly offset by abbreviation. — [ciphermysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/?p=2056); [ciphermysteries Averlino summary](https://ciphermysteries.com/2010/01/10/voynich-averlino-hypothesis-summary). In Pelling's counts, ol/al/or/ar pairs repeat back-to-back far more often than qo/ot/ok/op/of. He reads the doubled first group as genuine and possibly Roman numerals (cf. f15v "or or"), and the second group's doubles as copying errors. — [ciphermysteries post](https://ciphermysteries.com/?p=2910) (blog; single transcription; not peer-reviewed)
- **Pelling, *The Curse of the Voynich* (2006, Compelling Press).** The manuscript is an enciphered copy of Antonio Averlino (Filarete)'s lost books of secrets, made in Milan between 1456 and about 1480. He ties the "4o" token to mid-Quattrocento Northern Italian (Milanese chancery) cipher alphabets, and has also suggested a mixture of transposition and substitution. — [ciphermysteries Averlino summary](https://ciphermysteries.com/2010/01/10/voynich-averlino-hypothesis-summary); [ciphermysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/?p=772)
  - Reception: Augusto Buonafalce (Cryptologia 2008) rejected the Simonetta element, and Schmeh calls the Filarete hypothesis pseudo-scientific (as reported by Pelling). — [search summary of ciphermysteries posts](https://ciphermysteries.com/2008/01/11/the-curse-of-the-voynich-gets-cited)
  - Self-published, and the 1404–1438 vellum date conflicts with his 1450s–70s dating unless old vellum was reused.
- **Naibbe cipher (2024–2026 development).** Michael A. Greshko, "The Naibbe cipher: a substitution cipher that encrypts Latin and Italian as Voynich Manuscript-like ciphertext," *Cryptologia*, published online 26 Nov 2025, doi:10.1080/01611194.2025.2566408 (open access). Greshko is described as an independent researcher and science journalist. — [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/linguistics/voynich-manuscript-cipher-14466.html); [Archaeology Magazine](https://archaeologymag.com/2026/01/voynich-manuscript-may-be-a-cipher)
  - Method:
    - Type: a verbose homophonic substitution. Each plaintext letter maps to multiple distinct Voynichese glyph strings that follow an expanded slot grammar.
    - Splitting: plaintext is split into unigrams and bigrams, with dice controlling the split.
    - Tables: each chunk is enciphered from one of six tables, and a playing-card draw selects the table. One variant uses a 78-card tarocchi deck, the other a 52-card deck.
    - Feasibility: it can be done entirely by hand with early-15th-century Alpine-region materials, and the output stays fully decipherable. — [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/linguistics/voynich-manuscript-cipher-14466.html); [Schneier blog, quoting the abstract](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2025/12/substitution-cipher-based-on-the-voynich-manuscript.html) (the dice and six-table details come from blog commenters' summaries)
  - Statistics reproduced: symbol frequencies, word lengths, certain positional patterns, flattened frequencies and low entropy, glyph-pair behaviour. — [Archaeology Magazine](https://archaeologymag.com/2026/01/voynich-manuscript-may-be-a-cipher); [Voynich Ninja](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-5081-2.html)
  - Claims: the "ciphertext hypothesis … remains viable," and the work constrains plausible substitution structures. Greshko does not claim the manuscript is a ciphertext, and he calls the work a feasibility demonstration or benchmark. — [Schneier](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2025/12/substitution-cipher-based-on-the-voynich-manuscript.html); [Voynich Ninja](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-5081-2.html)
  - Stated limits:
    - Replication of Voynich B's properties is incomplete. — [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/linguistics/voynich-manuscript-cipher-14466.html)
    - It does not reproduce long-range correlations. Greshko conceded this.
    - Line and position effects were out of scope, so a positional mechanism would be needed if the manuscript is a ciphertext.
    - It generates only about 45% of Voynich word types and also produces non-Voynich types. Greshko conceded this.
    - The tables were filled with Voynich words, and their ratios were tuned to the manuscript. — [Voynich Ninja critique by Torsten (Timm) and replies by Greshko as "magnesium," Dec 7–8 2025](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-5081-2.html)
    - Media also quote Greshko that "in its current form, the Naibbe cipher fails in several major ways" (search-result snippet; the primary text was not checked).
  - Critique (Timm):
    - Circularity: success is built in because the cipher was fitted to the manuscript.
    - Long-range correlations may be structurally impossible for local randomized encryption, which could falsify the whole class.
    - The complexity is "an order of magnitude" beyond attested 15th-century ciphers.
    - Ambiguity handling makes reliable decryption doubtful.
    - Timm reads the failures as evidence against the ciphertext hypothesis. He is himself the author of a rival self-citation/meaningless-text model (Timm & Schinner). — [Voynich Ninja](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-5081-2.html)
  - Bruce Schneier blogged it in December 2025, and the comments there were mixed. — [Schneier](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2025/12/substitution-cipher-based-on-the-voynich-manuscript.html)

### Inferences
- **Polyalphabetic.** The strongest evidence is entropy and frequency shape. A periodic polyalphabetic cipher over Latin would raise h1 toward uniform, but Voynichese is skewed and highly predictable. I found no peer-reviewed paper that specifically tests Alberti or Vigenère, so "ruled out" rests on general cryptanalytic reasoning plus Wikipedia's summary.
- **Transposition.** Word- or line-level transposition of a natural language would preserve that language's letter-level entropy, which Voynichese lacks. Letter-level transposition would destroy the stable prefix-root-suffix slots that Tiltman found. Either way it is weak as a sole mechanism. This is my reasoning; I found no dedicated professional source.
- **Nomenclator/codebook.** Small-alphabet code groups (a code written in a 20–25 glyph script with slot structure) are not excluded. Repeated identical words ("up to five in a row") are awkward for a codebook but natural for a verbose cipher of numerals or of repeated letters.
- **Verbose homophonic.** Naibbe shows that such a cipher can be both hand-executable and decipherable, but the fit is partly by construction. Its failures, Currier's line effects and long-range correlations, are exactly the professional observations that remain unexplained.

- I found no other 2024–2026 Cryptologia Voynich papers in search results. Coverage may be incomplete.
- The CEUR paper's authors and details are unverified.

## Encodings feasible in the 15th century that remain consistent with the statistics

### Takeaway
What survives is a set of compound systems. The sources point to: verbose (multi-glyph-per-letter) substitution, possibly homophonic and possibly with abbreviation; constructed or a priori languages (Friedman); and systems with a positional or line-level component (Currier). Hoax or meaningless generation remains a strong competitor (Rugg; Timm & Schinner; Gaskell & Bowern). Nothing is established.

### Cited Findings
- **Verbose homophonic substitution** with randomizers (dice and cards) is shown to be feasible with early-15th-century materials and to reproduce many statistics. — [Sci.News on Greshko 2025](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/linguistics/voynich-manuscript-cipher-14466.html)
- **Verbose pairs plus abbreviation**, situated in Milanese chancery cipher practice, is Pelling's self-published hypothesis. — [ciphermysteries Averlino summary](https://ciphermysteries.com/2010/01/10/voynich-averlino-hypothesis-summary)
- **Constructed a priori language** was Friedman's view. Tiltman discussed it as possible but doubted it, and Cave Beck's 1657 language was Friedman's comparison. — [ciphermysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/tag/john-tiltman); [Tablet](https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/arts-letters/articles/cracking-the-voynich-code). A priori philosophical languages are documented mainly from the 17th century (Beck, Wilkins). A 15th-century origin would therefore be anachronistic unless it was a private precursor. That point is my inference, not a sourced claim.
- **Meaningless-text competitors.**
  - Rugg (2003–04): a Cardan grille over prefix/stem/suffix tables. Its weakness is that the grille postdates the vellum.
  - Timm & Schinner: a self-citation method.
  - Gaskell & Bowern (2022): human-written gibberish shares many Voynich statistics. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
  - Countervailing evidence: Montemurro (2013) found semantic structure, and Yale researchers (2021) found topic clusters that match the illustrations. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)

### Inferences
- A viable 15th-century cipher must do several things at once: lower h2 from about 3.2 to about 2.2, generate Tiltman-style slot words, produce Currier's A/B split (perhaps as two keys or table sets) and his line-initial/final effects, and allow exact word repeats. Verbose systems with positional rules meet more of these constraints than any other cipher family.

### Gaps
- I found no source that quantitatively tests codebooks or nomenclators against Voynich statistics.

## Why professional codebreakers failed (their own retrospectives)

### Takeaway
Professionals attributed failure to:
- having no crib or known plaintext and no parallel text;
- an uncertain glyph inventory and uncertain word and line segmentation, so the transliteration itself may encode wrong assumptions;
- statistics unlike any language, which defeat standard frequency methods;
- a structure (words that are "not words," functional lines) that implies an unknown intermediate step;
- poor early transcriptions and tooling.

### Cited Findings
- Elizebeth Friedman (1962): statistical analyses are "doomed to utter frustration." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- Currier: "there has to be an intermediate step somewhere"; "they're _not words!_"; "I can't interpret the data!" — [voynich.nu Currier](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/curr_main.html)
- Tiltman admitted he could identify no basis for a solution. — [ciphermysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/tag/john-tiltman)
- Transcription dependence:
  - Strong's method shows how one transliteration error changes everything. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html)
  - Alphabets differ (Friedman FSG, Currier, Bennett, EVA), so there is no consolidated frequency set, and glyph-composition choices shift entropy. — [voynich.nu a2_char](https://voynich.nu/a2_char.html)
  - Reeds found the 1962–63 printout nearly illegible. — [Voynich Ninja](https://www.voynich.ninja/printthread.php?tid=4036) (forum)
- Hidden assumptions: Rugg's lasting contribution is the point that analysts' unexamined assumptions, such as assuming the text is meaningful, may block a solution. — [voynich.nu solvers](https://www.voynich.nu/solvers.html)
- Unreproducible methods: D'Imperio's HMM result held, but Reddy & Knight's HMM result depends on initial conditions and was not reproduced by Zandbergen. — [voynich.nu a2_char](https://voynich.nu/a2_char.html)
- Statistics alone may not break a Voynich-style cipher, and knowing the intended reader or a probable word would help, i.e. a crib. — [Clive Robinson, Schneier blog comments](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2025/12/substitution-cipher-based-on-the-voynich-manuscript.html) (blog comment)

### Inferences
- The absence of a crib is decisive for the surviving hypotheses. A verbose homophonic cipher with randomized table choice has a very large key space relative to about 38,000 tokens. Without a probable word, the decipherment would be under-determined in the same way that sank Newbold and Strong.
