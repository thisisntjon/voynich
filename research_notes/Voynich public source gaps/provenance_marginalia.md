## Which letters and catalogue entries are transcribed in the open, and where are the originals?

### Takeaway
The open chain runs from a lost 1637 approach, through letters of 1639-1641 and the Marci gift letter dated 19 August 1665 in the open transcriptions, to Kinner's chasers of 1666 and 1667. The decipherment notes Marci says he enclosed have not survived. The 600-ducat Rudolf story is one sentence of hearsay inside the 1665 letter and does not name Mnishovsky, a seller, or a year.

### Cited Findings
- Johannes Marcus Marci to Athanasius Kircher, dateline in Philip Neal's English translation: "Prague 19 August 1665." Shelfmark Beinecke MS 408A (the letter alone). Neal: "This book was left to me by a close friend in his will"; "Doctor Raphael, the Czech language tutor of King Ferdinand III as they both then were, once told me that the said book belonged to Emperor Rudolph and that he presented 600 ducats to the messenger who brought him the book. He, Raphael, thought that the author was Roger Bacon the Englishman. I suspend my judgement on the matter." The letter does not name the friend, the seller, or Mnishovsky. Zandbergen: written by an unidentified scribe; the signature looks like Marci's; eyesight had failed. Scans of recto and verso are the Beinecke record Zandbergen links as https://brbl-dl.library.yale.edu/vufind/Record/3547362?image_id=1170955. Latin and English: http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_kircher_latin/#beinecke and http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1665_english/. Zandbergen's own page: https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html. Access: open web. voynich.nu footer on the index is "Copyright René Zandbergen, 2004-2026," not a reuse licence. Neal's footer has no licence line. The Beinecke overview page is "© 2026 Yale University Library. All Rights Reserved." — [Neal 1665 English](http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1665_english/); [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html); [Zandbergen archives](https://www.voynich.nu/archive.html); [Beinecke overview](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript)
- **Catalogue conflict, new vs the letter:** the same Beinecke overview still says Marci sent the book to Kircher "in 1666" and that Voynich bought it in 1912 "from the Jesuit College at Frascati." — [Beinecke overview](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript)
- Georgius Baresch (he signs "M. Georgius Baresch") to Kircher, 21 April 1639, APUG 557, fol. 353, Archivio della Pontificia Università Gregoriana, Rome. Open transcription and translation are Philip Neal's, first published by Smolka and Zandbergen (2010); the journal title was not on the page opened. Zandbergen quotes: "From the pictures of herbs, of which there are a great many in the codex, and of varied images, stars and other things bearing the appearance of chemical symbolism, it is my guess that the whole thing is medical" and "I ordered a certain old book to be transcribed in part, with the writing closely imitated." He had written about a year and a half earlier (so late 1637). Images as linked: http://archimede.imss.fi.it/kircher/557/large/353r.jpg, 353v.jpg, and 354v.jpg (wrapper). Whether that host still serves the files was not checked. — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html)
- Lost: Moretus to Kircher, late 1637, which carried Baresch's partial copy. Known only because the 1639 letters mention it. — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html)
- Kircher to Theodor Moretus, 12 March 1639. Original glued into Moretus's notebook, National Library of the Czech Republic, MS VI B 12b, fol. 73 (earlier fol. 70). Found by Josef Smolka. Kircher says he received the writing and had not read it. Neal's transcription revises the one in Smolka and Zandbergen (2010). A scan is on https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html (img/extra/lett_39a.gif). — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html)
- Marci to Kircher, 12 September 1640, APUG 557, fol. 127r (image http://archimede.imss.fi.it/kircher/557/large/127r.jpg). Recommends Baresch ("certe vir optimus") and encloses another Baresch attachment. Marci to Kircher, 12 January 1641, APUG 557, fol. 64r, mentions Baresch only in passing. English: http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1640_english/ and http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1641_english/. — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html)
- The "1662-65 notes" are not a surviving dossier. Marci says he is sending the friend's attempts; Zandbergen: "These notes have not been preserved." — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html)
- Godefrid Aloys Kinner to Kircher, 4 January 1666, APUG 562, fol. 138, and 5 January 1667, APUG 562, fol. 151. Images: http://archimede.imss.fi.it/kircher/562/large/138r.jpg (and 138v, 151r, 151v). Zandbergen's paraphrase: Kinner asks, for Marci, whether Kircher has progressed with the book sent the year before. The 1667 letter is his source for the carrier being the Bohemian provincial, Fr. Daniel Krupsky, in 1665. Neal's transcriptions are linked from the letters page; those two English pages were not opened. — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html)
- What the chain supports, and no further: a Prague owner matching the manuscript's pictures by April 1639 (Baresch); Marci holding it by 1665 and sending it to Kircher in Rome; a Dr Raphael, Czech tutor to Ferdinand as King of Bohemia, as the source of the Rudolf and Bacon remarks. It does not support a named seller, a purchase year, "600 ducats" as the price of this one book, or that Kircher answered. Zandbergen notes some authors treated Kircher's silence as evidence he never got the book; Kinner's letters are the counter-evidence that it was sent. — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html); [Neal](http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1665_english/)
- Baresch's death: Zandbergen, "between 1646 and 1662." Davis writes "Baresch's death in 1662." The Beinecke overview prints "Georgius Barschius (1585-1622)," which cannot survive the 1639 letter. — [Zandbergen letters](https://www.voynich.nu/letters.html); [Davis, 8 Sep 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/); [Beinecke overview](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript)
- Raphael Mnishovsky is an identification, not a name in the letter. Zandbergen uses "Raphael Mnisovsky" on the Rudolf essay and says he spoke with Marci while Baresch still owned the book. Neal's translation says only "Doctor Raphael." — [Zandbergen, sp_history](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/sp_history.html); [Neal](http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1665_english/)

### Inferences
- The earliest secure document is the 12 March 1639 Kircher reply, which presupposes the lost 1637 letter. Anything before that (Rudolf, Tepenec, a French reader) is not in this correspondence.
- A separate "1662-1665 notebook" should not be cited. The only candidate writing from Marci's ownership that is still on the object is the f1r margin, and that attribution is palaeographical, not a signed note.

### Gaps
- Smolka and Zandbergen 2010: journal, volume, and pages not copied from the letters page.
- Neal's notes on whether the year-line has been disputed (http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1665_notes/) were not opened. The 1665 vs 1666 conflict above is between his translation and the Beinecke prose page.
- The IMSS image host and the old Beinecke VuFind links were not fetched. An archive.org scan of an earlier printing (Newbold 1928 or similar) was not opened.
- No licence statement was found for the APUG photographs or for Neal's text.

## What did the 2009-2014 radiocarbon and codicological work do to the Rudolf II purchase story?

### Takeaway
The 2009 test dates the animals, not a purchase. It makes a book made for Rudolf, or made by Dee or Kelley on new parchment, very hard, and it leaves a later purchase of an old book untouched. The Dee sale has been dropped on documentary grounds. The 600-ducat purchase is still an assumption plus one account entry that does not name this manuscript. The library's own public page has not absorbed the carbon date.

### Cited Findings
- University of Arizona, announced at a press conference on 8 December 2009 for an ORF documentary. Zandbergen: parchment "sometime between 1404 and 1438 with 95% probability." He knows of no journal paper. An internal report was later released, at Rich Santacoloma's request; he hosts "a PDF copy" on voynich.nu. The relative path was not extracted. Greg Hodgins gave the fractions at the Villa Mondragone centenary, 11 May 2012: f8 (Herbal A, other half of the Tepenec bifolio) 0.9409±0.0044; f26 (Herbal B) 0.9380±0.0041; f47 (Herbal A, thick) 0.9389±0.0041; f68 (astronomical foldout) 0.9338±0.0041. His uncalibrated central years before 1950: 1461, 1436, 1444, 1400, each ±35 or ±38. The four agree with each other. The date is the death of the animal, not the writing. Zandbergen says the press-conference claim that the ink went on immediately "did not" follow from the analysis. — [Zandbergen, carbon](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- **Local claim, confirmed:** the figure people quote, 1404-1438 at 95%, is the public calibrated range on that page. It is not a writing date and not a place.
- What that does to Rudolf: a purchase around 1600 would be of skins about 160-200 years old. It does not confirm or deny the purchase. It does hit any story in which the book was manufactured in Rudolf's reign on contemporary parchment. — [Zandbergen, carbon](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- Dee as the seller is a separate retirement. Zandbergen summarises Rafal Prinke, published as Zandbergen and Prinke (2011, 2016): the three props fail. Folio 8s in the manuscript start at the bottom; Dee's and Kelley's start at the top. Arthur Dee's "hieroglyphics" memory is a 1675 Thomas Browne letter to Ashmole, and Arthur was nine when the family left Bohemia. The 17 October 1586 diary line of 630 ducats is Dee handing 800 florins to Francesco Pucci, not a receipt from Rudolf, and Dee had already been banished. The year 1586 "derives entirely" from Voynich's hypothesis. Soyga is a different book, found by Harkness. — [Zandbergen, sp_history](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/sp_history.html)
- Who still holds a piece of the purchase. Zandbergen, on that same essay: "I am assuming that Rudolf II owned the MS, and that, indeed, it was sold to him by Karl Widemann." On the history page the nearest account is March 1599, Rudolf buying books from the Augsburg physician Karl Widemann for 500 Taler, "close to 600 gold florins." A 25 February 1600 court note (Zacharias Geizkofler) concerns a barrel of "allerlai selzamen büchern" arrested in Augsburg and sent to Hans Popp. He also writes that 600 ducats "is entirely possible" as the price of a set, not of this book alone. Rauwolf as the previous owner is labelled "Speculation" and "there is no evidence." — [Zandbergen, history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html); [Zandbergen, sp_history](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/sp_history.html)
- Stefan Guzy, "Book transactions of Emperor Rudolf II 1576-1612. New findings on the earliest ownership of the Voynich manuscript," 2022 Voynich conference, Malta. Open PDF: https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/paper16.pdf. Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYscfv43y7E. The PDF was not opened (no books or long PDFs downloaded). Print follow-up: Guzy, "Rudolf II und das väßl mitt allerlai selzamen büchern," Herold-Jahrbuch, n.s. 28 (2023), Selbstverlag Herold, Berlin. Not open. — [Zandbergen, refs](https://www.voynich.nu/refs.html)
- Independent of the price: the f1r ex libris of Jacobus de Tepenec, a Rudolf courtier 1608-1611, is why a Prague court connection in those years does not rest on Marci. — [Zandbergen, history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- Catalogue lag, **new vs the carbon page:** Beinecke's current overview still says the manuscript "was produced in Central Europe at the end of the fifteenth or during the sixteenth century" and that "scientific testing suggests material consistency with manuscripts produced in the fifteenth century." It does not state 1404-1438. — [Beinecke overview](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript)
- Jesuit hiding place, **against the Frascati sentence on that page:** Zandbergen says the Mondragone story came from H. P. Kraus, and that Lorenzo Mancini's work puts the rescued Collegium Romanum books, including the Kircher correspondence (APUG), at Villa Torlonia in Castel Gandolfo. — [Zandbergen, history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)

### Inferences
- Defended piece: a Rudolf-circle ownership after 1608 (Tepenec), plus a possible 1599 Widemann lot (Guzy's subject; Zandbergen's working assumption). Dropped piece: Dee/Kelley as the sellers, and 1586 as the year. Not dropped, and not proved: that Rudolf paid 600 ducats for this codex.
- Nobody opened in this pass retracts Rudolf ownership altogether. Absence of such a retraction was not searched past these pages.

### Gaps
- Guzy's PDF and the 2011/2016 Prinke papers were not opened, so his exact claim (one book vs a lot; florins vs ducats) is not quoted from him.
- The 2014 York calfskin identification and Paula Zyats's binding report were not re-opened. They are not used above.
- Hodgins's upper bound, where Zandbergen says the formal figures differ slightly, was in the unread part of the carbon page.

## Open editions of the f116v, f17r, and f1r writing

### Takeaway
There is still no typed, agreed transcription of any of the three. Zandbergen gives a month-name table and a one-line description of f116v. Davis (2024) assigns the f1r alphabets to Marci and calls f116v/f17r Germanic and roughly contemporary, without a word reading in the sentences extracted. Pelling (22 Jan 2026) offers an Occitan reading of f17r and corrects one word to "meilhor."

### Cited Findings
- **f1r columns.** Zandbergen's table: "f1r SEQ V+L. Three vertical sequences of single characters in the right margin, faded or erased. Tentatively identified as the hand of Johannes Marcus Marci." He calls them "presumably a decryption attempt by a later owner." — [Zandbergen, writing](https://www.voynich.nu/writing.html)
- Davis, 8 Sep 2024, same page of images from the 2014 Lazarus capture: a Roman alphabet in the right margin, legible under ultraviolet, "a, b, c, d, and e" clear in visible light; "two Roman alphabets" in "Humanistic bookhand." "Are these alphabets an early attempt to decode the manuscript? Perhaps." She compared hands of Rauwolf, Rudolf, Sinapius, Baresch, Marci, Kircher, Dee, and Kelley. "One of these men is a very good match: Johannes Marcus Marci (1595-1667)." She dates his ownership from Baresch's death in 1662 to the 1665 gift. The post's preliminary table is an image; no typed glyph-to-letter list was in the text extracted. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- **Local claim, corrected in wording, not in substance:** "the f1r alphabet column is attributed to Marci" matches Davis's "very good match" and Zandbergen's "tentatively." It does not support an authorial key, a clean row alignment, or a unique pairing (two alphabets). Forum dissent was not re-read, by design.
- **f116v and f17r.** Zandbergen: f17r has "a largely unreadable comment near the top margin"; Pelling first saw one or two Voynich words under UV; he points to Pelling (2006) p. 164 and does not print a normalized string (the page's glyphs did not survive as text). f116v is "a short paragraph of text including what appears to be German, Latin and two words in the Voynich script," plus a line in the top margin. Table row: "Recipe or spell, in a mixture of pseudo-Latin and German, with two words in Voynich script." — [Zandbergen, writing](https://www.voynich.nu/writing.html)
- Davis: "The writing is similar to, but isn't quite, Voynichese. It has Germanic features and looks roughly contemporary with the manuscript, although it might be a bit later. There is a similar inscription in the upper margin of f. 17r, likely written by the same hand. No one really knows what these inscriptions represent, or when they were written, or where, or why." — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- **Local claim, corrected:** the open wording is Davis's "Germanic features" and Zandbergen's "pseudo-Latin and German." The label "pseudo-Germanic" was not in either passage. It should not be cited as their term from these pages.
- Pelling, 22 Jan 2026: he says he can read "the f17r marginalia." On one word: "the first word actually seemed to have originally been 'meilhor'." He treats the line as Occitan (the post's frame). "Mallier," "aller," "balsamina," and "otaiol" were not in the slices extracted, so they are not quoted. — [Pelling, 22 Jan 2026](https://ciphermysteries.com/2026/01/22/finally-i-can-read-the-voynich-manuscript)
- Named disagreement: Davis and Zandbergen describe a Germanic or German-Latin mixture and do not print a sentence. Pelling prints at least "meilhor" and reads Occitan. No palaeographer's word-for-word edition of f116v was found on the open pages opened. Davis is the palaeographer in this set; her published comment is the hand and the language character, not a transcription.
- **Zodiac glosses, local claim corrected.** They are month-names. Zandbergen's table: f70v2 Pisces mars; f70v1 and f71r Aries aberil; f71v and f72r1 Taurus may; f72r2 Gemini jong; f72r3 Cancer iollet; f72v3 Leo augst; f72v2 Virgo septe(m)b(r); f72v1 Libra octe(m)bre; f73r Scorpius nove(m)bre; f73v Sagittarius decebre. "Certainly a Romance language"; he prefers Northern French, with Spanish and Occitan as candidates he sets aside. Added "in a later hand." Capricorn and Aquarius are not in the table. — [Zandbergen, writing](https://www.voynich.nu/writing.html). The local finding that no Voynich nymph-label has been matched to a month name is a different claim and was not retested.

### Inferences
- Usable as evidence of readers: a later Romance hand naming months; a near-contemporary hand mixing Latin script and a few Voynich words; a 17th-century humanistic hand, very likely Marci's, trying alphabets. None of the three is an authorial plaintext crib in the sources opened.
- Building a cipher test from f1r still requires transcribing Davis's image by hand. Nobody on these pages has published that table as text.

### Gaps
- Davis's full f116v letter-by-letter comments, including any "u or v" revision, were not in the slices extracted. Not confirmed and not denied.
- Pelling's full f17r line and his 5 Apr 2026 Occitan notes were not extracted.
- Zandbergen's f116v and marginalia URLs that 404'd in the local notes still have no replacement word edition on writing.html.

## Which 2017-2026 books state an external prediction rather than a glossary?

### Takeaway
One book-length claim was verified from a secondary page, and it is a place, a date, and a map, not a glossary. It conflicts with the carbon date. Other famous 2017-2020 claims that turned up are articles. A full eight-book list was not justified by the pages opened.

### Cited Findings
- Jules Janick and Arthur O. Tucker, *Unraveling the Voynich Codex*, Springer, 2018, ISBN 978-3-319-77293-6. Wikipedia, not the book (not downloaded): they argue from plant and animal identifications, and from a "kabbalah map of central Mexico (folio 86v)," that the manuscript "was composed in Mexico between 1562 and 1572." Test: folio 86v should be that map; the plants should be New World species; the composition date should fall in 1562-1572. The last conflicts with parchment dated 1404-1438. No review by a named manuscript scholar was opened. The Wikipedia plant-page sentence that botanical opinion is against a sunflower on f93r is a different citation and is not a review of this book. — [Wikipedia, Voynich manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript); [Zandbergen, carbon](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- Not books, so not counted, but they are the other dated public predictions the same page actually names: Gerard Cheshire, "The language and writing system of MS 408 (Voynich) explained," *Romance Studies* 37 (2019): 30-67, doi 10.1080/02639904.2019.1599566, a "calligraphic proto-Romance" reading, plus 2020 plant notes naming *Andromeda polifolia* and *Paris quadrifolia* (academia.edu, not a book). Ahmet Ardic and Ozan Ardic, "Voynich Manuscript: Revealed Turkic Origin," *Turkic Research* 1, no. 1 (2020), a language identification, not confirmed here as book-length. Nicholas Gibbs is a Wikipedia section heading only in what was extracted; no 2017 book title was on the page slice. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)
- Not solutions: Guzy 2022/2023 (ownership) and Layfield and Davis, "Singulion Structure and the Voynich Manuscript," *Digital Medievalist* 19 (2026), doi 10.4000/16k0a, a binding and order argument. The article was not opened. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript); [Zandbergen, refs](https://www.voynich.nu/refs.html)

### Inferences
- Against the Janick and Tucker date, the carbon result is decisive unless they explicitly use old blank parchment. That escape was not checked in their book.
- A glossary-only "solution" published in this decade was not collected. The bar set for this note excluded them on purpose.

### Gaps
- No publisher catalogue was searched. Books that meet the bar and are missing here are a search failure, not evidence they do not exist. Springer was not opened; the ISBN is Wikipedia's.
- Scholarly responses to Janick and Tucker, Cheshire, and Ardic were not opened, so none is reported.
