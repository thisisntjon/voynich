## Bibliographic (verified via Crossref)

Keagan Brewer & Michelle L. Lewis, "The Voynich Manuscript, Dr Johannes
Hartlieb and the Encipherment of Women's Secrets", Social History of
Medicine 37(3):559–582. Published online 2024-03-22; print 2024-11-02.
Publisher OUP. PDF link in Crossref record (access-controlled).

## What the paper actually claims (abstract, verbatim scope)

1. Investigates the culture of self-censorship, erasure and encipherment of
   women's secrets, focused on Dr Johannes Hartlieb (c.1410–68).
2. Hartlieb apprehended propagation of women's secrets in vernacular
   Bavarian, culminating in a call for 'secret letters' to hide recipes
   for abortifacients and contraceptives.
3. Other encipherment cases re intercourse/genitalia described.
4. Proposal: the Rosettes illustration represents coitus and conception.
No decipherment, no cipher-mechanism claim for Voynichese text, no solver
prior in the abstract. It is a historical-context + iconography paper.

## Secondary detail (not primary-verified)

Author-reported (The Conversation, via press summaries): the authors decoded
multiple period ciphered writings; longest a 21-line late-medieval N. Italian
cipher obscuring a gynaecological (incl. abortion) recipe. Cipher types,
alphabets, and abbreviation habits of those comparators are NOT in the
abstract and remain unverified until full text or the 2022 CEUR talk paper
(Brewer, VMS Talk) is opened at its CEUR original.

secret-letters call is a documented period motive for enciphering exactly
this genre in exactly this region/date — legitimately kept.

## Usable priors for the team (and their limits)

- Motive prior (weak, contextual): encipherment of vernacular medical
  secrets was explicitly advocated in Bavaria c.1450s. Supports taking
  "deliberately enciphered vernacular" seriously; constrains nothing
  statistically. Note date tension: Hartlieb's call postdates the
  1404–1438 parchment window's midpoint — practice may predate the call,
  but the paper (per abstract) argues culture, not direct authorship.
- Language prior (weak): vernacular Bavarian/German is the at-risk medium
  in Hartlieb's account. Team solvers already tested German (negative at
  letter level, a6b); Bavarian dialect corpora untested — marginal value.
- Comparator ciphers (potentially strong, currently unverified): if the
  decoded period ciphers use tables/abbreviations relevant to K4-style
  shared codewords, they become control material. Requires full text.
- Rosettes-as-conception: iconographic hypothesis; no scoreboard metric
  currently tests it. Park unless M-series image work needs hypotheses.
