# Physical, Codicological and Provenance Evidence on the Voynich Manuscript (Beinecke MS 408), and How It Constrains Decipherment Theories

Notes compiled 2026-10-17 14:58 PDT (clock read from system). Coverage through mid-October 2026. Findings dated 2024-2026 are tagged **[2024]**, **[2026]**, and so on. Sourcing is mostly voynich.nu (René Zandbergen), Lisa Fagin Davis's blog, the 2026 Digital Medievalist article, and press coverage. Forum and blog material is labelled as such.

## Q1. Radiocarbon dating (2009) and the McCrone ink analysis: what each does and does not prove

### Takeaway
The parchment is firmly early 15th century. Four folios were tested and give tightly consistent dates, roughly 1404-1438 at 95%. The ink and pigment analysis found materials consistent with the period and "no modern components." Neither test dates the act of writing. Theories of a much later origin must therefore assume that blank old vellum sat unused for a long time. That is not impossible, but it is improbable. An outright modern forgery, such as one by Voynich himself, faces a heavy burden. Nothing rules out a gap of a few decades between the skins and the ink.

### Cited Findings
- The University of Arizona dated the parchment in 2009 as part of an ORF (Austrian TV) documentary. Greg Hodgins presented the results, including at the May 2012 Villa Mondragone centenary event. — [voynich.nu carbon dating page](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- Four folios were sampled. They were f8 (Herbal-A, the leaf with the Tepenec ex libris), f26 (Herbal-B), f47 (Herbal-A, thick parchment) and f68 (an astronomical foldout). The samples therefore span both Currier "languages" and several sections. — [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- The public 95% range was 1404-1438. Zandbergen's own combined calculation gives 1404-1435, and the combined uncalibrated age is 1434 ± 18. The four measurements agree closely (mean fraction 0.9379, SD 0.0030, smaller than the per-sample errors of about 0.004), so there is "no indication the samples come from different ages." — [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- Zandbergen notes that radiocarbon gives the date the animal died and the parchment was made, not the date of writing. The gap before inking and the time needed to finish the book are unknown. Later entries, such as the zodiac month names, are possible. Very-late theories must dispute the result or argue that the parchment sat unused for a century or more, which falls outside the 95% range though it is not impossible in principle. — [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- At the 2009 press conference it was said that the inks were applied immediately after the parchment was made. Zandbergen calls this incorrect, because the analysis "did not provide any information about this." — [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- In 2014 the University of York identified the parchment as calf skin (vellum in the strict sense) and the cover as goat skin. — [voynich.nu description page](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)
- The McCrone Associates materials analysis is dated 1 April 2009 and was published by the Beinecke. As reported, it identified vellum, iron gall inks, azurite, a copper-based green and gum. A Yale conservation write-up states that drawing and writing inks appear to be from the same period and that "there are no modern components in the volume." — [Univ. of Toronto Omeka record linking the report PDF](https://omeka.ischool.utoronto.ca/items/show/463); [AIC Conservators Converse](https://resources.culturalheritage.org/conservators-converse/tag/carbon-14-dating/)
- Amateur critics on forums argue three points: McCrone's instruments showed only that the ink contains iron rather than positively identifying iron-gall ink; the green pigment was characterised only as copper-bearing and non-crystalline; and one red pigment may have been misidentified. These are forum claims, not peer-reviewed rebuttals. A claimed titanium compound in an ink sample on f70v could not be confirmed against the report. — [Voynich Ninja thread](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-3808-5.html)
- **[2024]** Davis takes the C-14 date (parchment about 1425) as making the manuscript an early-15th-century book, while noting that "not everyone agrees." She argues that faded, chemically treated early annotations on f1r tell against a modern forgery. — [Davis, Manuscript Road Trip, 8 Sep 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- **[2024]** Multispectral imaging found no palimpsest or underwriting on the ten imaged leaves. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)

### Inferences
- **Effectively ruled out:** a modern forgery by Voynich, or any post-1600 creation, since that would need about 200 blank folios of 1400s calf vellum and period-consistent pigments. A Roger Bacon authorship (13th century) is also excluded unless the text is a later copy; the parchment is about 150 years too late for Bacon himself. A Dee/Kelley or Kelley-forgery fabrication of the 1580s becomes very unlikely: it would require large stocks of 150-year-old blank vellum. The absence of a palimpsest removes the "recycled old vellum" escape route in its usual form. Any New World or "Nahuatl" origin, for example Tucker and Talbert, is chronologically very hard, since it would need writing more than 60-100 years after the skins were prepared.
- **Supported:** an early-to-mid 15th-century European production. The agreement of samples from Herbal-A, Herbal-B and the astronomical foldout implies that one parchment stock served for the whole book. That supports a single production campaign rather than a composite.
- **Not proven by the tests:** the exact writing date, the place, and whether the writing is meaningful. A 15th-century hoax, such as Gordon Rugg's table-and-grille hypothesis, is not excluded by the material evidence.

### Gaps
- I did not read the McCrone report PDF directly. The listed materials are taken from secondary summaries.
- I did not locate the University of Arizona's 2011 press release itself (unverified). Hodgins's formal publication figures, which Zandbergen says differ slightly at the upper limit, were not checked.

## Q2. Davis's five scribes (Manuscript Studies, 2020) and the mapping to Currier's Languages A and B

### Takeaway
Davis (2020) identified five hands by palaeography. Scribe 1 corresponds to Currier Language A (Herbal-A and most Pharma). The other scribes write Language B, with one Pharma exception. Nearly every bifolio is the work of a single scribe. Multiple hands that are statistically distinct and tied to the physical structure argue strongly against "one lone genius" solutions. Any decipherment must also explain why its text behaves differently by scribe or dialect. The five-scribe model is contested by some researchers.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Lisa Fagin Davis, "How Many Glyphs and How Many Scribes? Digital Paleography and the Voynich Manuscript," *Manuscript Studies* 5, no. 1 (2020): 164-180 (University of Pennsylvania Press). The volume, issue and pages are as cited in Layfield & Davis 2026. — [Layfield & Davis, Digital Medievalist 19 (2026)](https://journals.openedition.org/digitalmedievalist/2331)
- Currier, a cryptologist working in the 1970s, proposed two statistically distinct "languages" and 5-8 scribes. He tied Scribe 1 to Language A and the other scribes to Language B. Davis's work is described as the first elaboration of Currier's scribe observation. — [Goodreads, Voynich Reconsidered blog](https://goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24890557-voynich-reconsidered-scribes-and-languages); [Layfield & Davis 2026](https://journals.openedition.org/digitalmedievalist/2331)
- The mapping as tabulated in Layfield & Davis 2026 (Table 1):
  - Botanical section: Scribes 1, 2, 3 and 5 (Languages A and B).
  - Cosmological section: Scribe 4 (B).
  - Biological or balneological section: Scribe 2 (B).
  - Pharmaceutical section: Scribes 1 and 3 (A and B).

  — [Layfield & Davis 2026](https://journals.openedition.org/digitalmedievalist/2331)
- On 52 extant bifolia, all four pages were written by one scribe, with two exceptions: bifolia 57|66 and 85|86, the Rosettes foldout. Page 115r is shared by Scribes 2 and 3. — [Layfield & Davis 2026](https://journals.openedition.org/digitalmedievalist/2331)
- One differentiating feature: per Davis, Scribes 1, 3 and 5 write the k-gallows in one stroke, and Scribes 2 and 4 in two. A critic disputes this distinction. One blog reports that Davis prefers "dialect" to "language" and assigns the roughly 30 pages Currier left unassigned to dialect B. Another critic notes that Davis attributes nearly all Currier-A pages, including Pharma-A, to Scribe 1, whereas Currier attributed only Herbal-A to that scribe. — [Goodreads blog](https://goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24890557-voynich-reconsidered-scribes-and-languages) (blog, secondary)
- **[2026]** Torsten Timm (2026) argues that discrete scribe categories, like discrete language categories, are imposed on a continuum. He treats the five-scribe hypothesis as a "foundational premise" of recent research that deserves scrutiny. — reported via [Goodreads blog](https://goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24890557-voynich-reconsidered-scribes-and-languages); I did not read the paper itself.
- **[2026]** Davis: "the 'lone genius' idea is kind of out of the door"; the manuscript is "a more communal object, perhaps a set of booklets through which information was passed down." — [IFLScience, Oct 2026](https://www.iflscience.com/we-may-have-been-reading-the-voynich-manuscript-in-the-wrong-order-for-over-a-century-84870)

### Inferences
- **Weakened:** single-author schemes that are idiosyncratic and improvised, such as a one-person glossolalia, a private mental-illness script, or a single hoaxer. Theories positing several scribes who copy from an exemplar fit better. The one-scribe-per-bifolio pattern suggests parallel copying of unbound sheets.
- **A decipherment must account for the A/B split:** if A and B track scribes, the split may reflect scribal habit, a different encoding table or a different source text. Any claimed key that reads Herbal-A and Herbal-B the same way, without explaining the statistical difference, is suspect.
- If Timm's "continuum" critique holds, the A/B split may be gradual, for example a system that evolves over time. That fits "self-citation" or generative-process theories (Timm & Schinner) better than "two languages" theories.

### Gaps
- I could not access the 2020 article text (repository.upenn.edu returned 403). The per-folio scribe assignments and full list of letterform criteria come from secondary sources and were not verified against the article.

## Q3. Quire structure, original vs. current order, missing folios, foldouts (Rosettes), and evidence of rearrangement

### Takeaway
The book now has 102 of an original approximately 116 folios in 18 of about 20 quires. Several foldouts survive, including the large Rosettes sheet (f85/86). The current order is firmly not the original one. **[2026]** Layfield & Davis argue that the book was conceived as loose single bifolia ("singulions") meant to be read in sequence. On their account these were nested into quires in a single 15th-century sewing, and that sewing was itself the misbinding. The page order therefore cannot be assumed to be the author's, and any reading that depends on page continuity is suspect.

### Cited Findings
- Size is about 225 × 160 mm and about 5 cm thick. There are 102 folios now, from probably 116 originally (14 missing), and 18 quires now from probably 20 (Quires 16 and 18 entirely missing). A standard quire is 4 bifolios. — [voynich.nu description](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)
- Missing leaves: f12 (stub); ff59-64 (three central bifolios of Quire 8); f74 (stub, with cut marks visible on f75r); ff91-92 (Quire 16); ff97-98 (Quire 18); ff109-110 (centre of Quire 20). Capricorn and Aquarius are absent from the zodiac, probably on a lost page. — [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)
- **[2024]** Davis notes "at least sixteen leaves are missing." — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/) (the 14-vs-16 count differs between sources)
- Foldouts: some bifolios carry 3-5 panels. f72 is a multiple foldout. f85/86 is the large foldout, about 45 × 45 cm with an extra horizontal fold, carrying the Rosettes drawing. — [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)
- Folio numbers 1-116 appear at the top right of rectos and were written by a later owner. De Ricci (1937) dated them to the 16th century and Manly to the 17th from the handwriting, though a 15th-century date is not excluded. Quire marks (an Arabic numeral plus the "9" abbreviation for -us) are older in style, and Manly dated them to the 15th century. Quires 19-20 lack the trailing 9. Pelling suggests several hands in the quire marks. — [voynich.nu description](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html); [voynich.nu origin page](https://voynich.nu/extra/sp_origin.html)
- Zandbergen's firm conclusions: the present page order is not the original; quire numbers were added before folio numbers; the present binding came after both and after leaves had already gone missing. Herbal-A and Herbal-B bifolios are intermixed, yet the quire numbers run in sequence, so the quire numbers postdate the mix-up. Painting preceded the present binding: blue paint from f33v transferred to f40r. Paint over a folio number on f42r is tentative evidence of some later painting. — [voynich.nu origin page](https://voynich.nu/extra/sp_origin.html)
- The binding is a limp vellum (goat) cover, described by the Beinecke as 18th-19th century. It probably replaced an earlier cover under the Jesuits. Pastedowns and fillers were probably removed by Voynich. 1960s leather thongs were glued over older fibre thongs, which are possibly 15th-century originals. — [voynich.nu description](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)
- **[2026]** Layfield & Davis, "Singulion Structure and the Voynich Manuscript," *Digital Medievalist* 19 (online 10 July 2026, DOI 10.4000/16k0a). Their main points:
  - Method: Latent Semantic Analysis of the text, with the Aberdeen Bestiary and a Speculum Humanae Salvationis manuscript as controls. Conclusion: the VMS "is not meant to be bound in quires at all" but was a set of single bifolia read in sequence.
  - Conservator Paula Zyats (2016) found only one sewing, dated late Gothic (about 1400-1500). Before the present vellum covers there was one set of wooden covers, likely 15th-century. The original 15th-century binding was itself the misbinding.
  - Evidence that decoration came before binding: f78v streams continue on the non-consecutive conjoint f81r. Visual links run across conjoint pairs 10v|15r (splash stain), 33v|40r (blue petals), 58v|65r (green leaf) and 76v|83r (nymph's arm).
  - Enlarged initial glyphs on f76r and f105r mark section starts.
  - Tentative original orders are proposed for Quire 13 (balneological) and Quire 20 (stars), which "may be" correct but cannot be confirmed until the text is read.

  — [Layfield & Davis 2026](https://journals.openedition.org/digitalmedievalist/2331)
- **[2026]** Press coverage followed in early October 2026; the Guardian URL is dated 2026/oct/06 per IFLScience. — [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/we-may-have-been-reading-the-voynich-manuscript-in-the-wrong-order-for-over-a-century-84870); [UNN](https://unn.ua/en/news/the-mysterious-voynich-manuscript-may-have-been-read-in-the-wrong-order-for-centuries)
- **[2026]** Timm's critique on the Voynich Ninja forum accepts that elevated textual similarity within physical bifolia is "real and independently confirmed." He argues that the reading-order interpretation built on it is "underdetermined by the data." No independent replication of the reordering was found. — [Voynich Ninja thread 5911](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-5911.html) (forum)
- **[2024]** MSI of f93r shows text written over a pigment stain, which Davis takes as evidence that the artists worked before the scribes. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)

### Inferences
- **Ruled out or weakened:** decipherments that depend on reading across page turns in the current order, on running text continuing between non-conjoint leaves, or on positional or page-number keys tied to the current foliation. Theories that treat the foliation or quire numbers as authorial are also weakened.
- **Supported:** a production model in which a book was planned and illustrated as separate bifolia and then assembled by others. This is consistent with multiple scribes, one per bifolio. A "picture-first, text-second" workflow fits a copyist filling in text around existing images. A hoaxer composing freely could also produce it, so it is not decisive.
- Lost leaves, including two zodiac signs and the centre of Quire 8, mean that any "complete key" or any argument from the totals of the full text is drawn from an incomplete corpus.

### Gaps
- I did not find a detailed public description of the Rosettes foldout's physical construction (panel count, hinge sequence) in the sources I read.
- The full proposed singulion order from Layfield & Davis was not extracted. Davis's forthcoming book on the subject is reportedly about a year away, per the forum, unverified.

## Q4. Ownership chain, including 2020-2026 archival findings

### Takeaway
The chain is firm from Horčický (after 1608) onward, with gaps before Baresch and between Horčický and Baresch. The Rudolf II purchase rests on second-hand testimony in Marci's letter. **[2023]** It is now strengthened, though not proven, by Stefan Guzy's discovery of a 1599 sale of "rare books" by Carl Widemann to Rudolf for an amount matching "600." Nothing in the chain places the book in England or with Dee or Kelley, or before about 1599 at all.

### Cited Findings
Ownership table, per Zandbergen:

| Period | Owner or event | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early 15th c. | Unknown | No ownership evidence | Unknown |
| 1576-1611 reign | Rudolf II | Marci's 1665 letter reports that "Dr. Raphael" said the book had belonged to Rudolf, and that the bearer received 600 ducats | Hearsay |
| After 1608 | Jacobus Horčický de Tepenec (d. 1622) | Ex libris on f1r, faded or erased, visible under UV | Firm |
| To 1646 | Georg Baresch (Barschius) | His April 1639 letter to Kircher, the only surviving one, says he owned it; an earlier partial transcription was sent via Theodor Moretus in 1637, and that letter is lost | Firm |
| 1646-1662 | Johannes Marcus Marci | Inherited Baresch's alchemical library | Firm |
| 1665 | Athanasius Kircher | Marci's covering letter, dated 1665 (some sources say 1666) | Firm |
| c. 1665-1873 | Jesuit Collegium Romanum | Probable; rebound between 1824 and 1870 | Highly probable |

— [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- The page never explicitly identifies "Dr. Raphael" as Raphael Mnishovsky. It discusses Marci talking with Mnishovsky before 1644, and Raphael as tutor to Ferdinand III. The identification is implied, not stated. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- Typewritten "ex Bibliotheca privata P. Petri Beckx" labels (Beckx was Jesuit General) were on books in the Voynich-acquired collection. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- Wilfrid Voynich acquired the manuscript via Fr. Strickland under secrecy; the sale was completed in July 1912, though 1911 also appears in sources. On the location, Villa Mondragone was long believed, but **[2022]** research by Potenza and Mancini points to Villa Torlonia at Castel Gandolfo. Zandbergen says this analysis is under major update. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- Later transfers: in 1930 to Ethel Voynich (60%) and Anne Nill (40%); on 12 July 1961 to H. P. Kraus for $24,500. Kraus asked $160,000 and failed to sell, then donated it to Yale (Beinecke) in 1969. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- **[2024]** Multispectral imaging confirms Voynich's reading of the faded f1r inscription as "Jacobi à Tepenecz." Voynich tried to reveal it with chemicals around 1914. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- **[2022-2023]** Stefan Guzy (University of the Arts Bremen) published his findings in the proceedings of the 1st International Conference on the Voynich Manuscript (2022), and they were publicised in January 2023.
  - In imperial Hofkammer account journals (Vienna and Prague), out of about 7,000 entries, 126 concern books. Only one involves 600 gold coins: in 1599, physician Carl Widemann sold Rudolf a collection of manuscripts.
  - One record gives 500 silver thaler and another 600 florins. The books are described as "remarkable/rare" and transported in a small barrel.
  - Guzy argues that Marci's "ducats" loosely meant gold coins.
  - Widemann lived in the house of botanist Leonhard Rauwolf, who is a "possible candidate" for an earlier owner.
  - The article does **not** confirm that the VMS was in the Widemann lot; the link rests on price and timing.

  — [The History Blog, 15 Jan 2023](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66173); [CEUR-WS Vol-3313 paper16](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/paper16.pdf)
- Zandbergen adds that the Geizkofler family archive lists 600 Rheinische Gulden, and court records of 25 Feb 1600 mention "unusual" books sent to chamberlain Hans Popp. He rates the Widemann-to-Rudolf sale as "probable, not proven" and Rauwolf's prior ownership as unconfirmed. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- Sceptics note that 600 florins is plausible for a batch rather than a single book (forum). D. N. O'Donovan argues that Baresch's correspondence never mentions Rudolf (reader comment, 2024). — [Voynich Ninja thread 3929](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-3929.html); [The History Blog](https://www.thehistoryblog.com/archives/66173)
- The Dee/Kelley sale route, a 1921 hypothesis, is now rejected per Zandbergen. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)

### Inferences
- **Weakened:** Dee or Kelley as authors or sellers, and English-origin theories that rest on Dee. The Widemann/Rauwolf route points instead to Augsburg and southern Germany around 1599.
- **Ruled out:** Voynich himself as forger, since Baresch's 1639 letter and Marci's letter to Kircher describe the book and survive in the Kircher correspondence. A 17th-century creation by Baresch or Marci is also excluded, because the book predates them and Horčický owned it after 1608.
- **Weakened:** claims of a special Bacon connection. That idea originates in Marci's hearsay, which says Rudolf believed it was by Roger Bacon.
- The roughly 1430-1599 gap is total. Any theory naming an author must bridge 170 years with no documentary trace.

### Gaps
- I did not independently consult the Marci letter text or the Beinecke catalogue entry in this session. The Kircher correspondence (Baresch 1639) was not consulted directly.
- I found no 2024-2026 archival discoveries about ownership beyond the 2022-2023 Guzy, Potenza and Mancini work (absence of evidence, not confirmed absence).

## Q5. Non-Voynichese writing: f116v marginalia, zodiac month names, f1r "key" columns

### Takeaway
There are three later or extra-textual layers. First, the f116v (and f17r) marginalia are in a Latin-script-like hand with Germanic features, roughly contemporary with the manuscript. Second, the zodiac month names are in a Romance vernacular, debated as Occitan or northern French, possibly with some Germanic forms. They are a probable later 15th-century addition and so evidence of an early reader, not the author. Third, **[2024]** the f1r right-margin columns, two Latin alphabets and a column of Voynichese glyphs, which Davis attributes to Marci (1662-1665). These are an early decoding attempt, not an authorial key.

### Cited Findings
- **[2024]** f116v: the top-margin lines are similar to Voynichese but not identical, have "Germanic features," and are roughly contemporary with the manuscript or slightly later. MSI suggests that the third and fourth words begin with a letter other than the previously read "p," "perhaps [u] or [v]." Faint descending lines are stains. Show-through from f116r adds noise, so readings remain tentative. Davis offers no interpretation. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- **[2024]** The f17r upper-margin inscription is likely in the same hand as f116v. No transcription is given. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- Zodiac month names, as read by Schwerdtfeger, include forms such as "mars," "aberil," "may" and "octembre," which look "a sort of French." Some call them Occitan, while more cautious readers suggest northern French. Bax notes a 14th-century Picard astrolabe with similar names. — [Stephen Bax blog](https://stephenbax.net/?p=1226)
- Pelling's Occitan case: the closest match is the livre de raison of Jaume Deydier, a farmer of Ollioules near Toulon, written 1477-1521. — [Cipher Mysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/tag/occitan)
- Dating and hand of the month names: Toresella and a palaeographer consulted by Stolfi place them in a 15th-century hand, possibly several decades later than the main text. One forum argument holds that the March-September names fit an Alemannic/Germanic line while October-December end in "-re," which is more Romance. That would suggest different annotators, though it is disputed. — [Voynich Ninja thread 5299](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-5299.html) (forum)
- Zandbergen describes the month names as Romance, "probably Northern French," and added later. — [voynich.nu history](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html)
- **[2024]** f1r right margin: three columns. Column 1 is a Roman alphabet a-z, column 2 a sequence of Voynichese characters, and column 3 a second Roman alphabet offset by one letter. The columns were imaged in 2014 by the Lazarus Project (Phelps, Heyworth, Van Duzer, Boydston, Easton) and made public in September 2024.
  - Attribution: Davis attributes the hand to Marci by letterform comparison with his autograph letters. Her markers include loopless b, d, f, h, p, q, s and y, an open-bowl g, a distinctive m and the shape of z. This dates the columns to 1662-1665.
  - Interpretation: possibly a substitution-cipher attempt; Davis considers the columns unlikely to help decipherment.
  - Other observations: two Voynichese "majuscule" glyphs occur only on f1r, and UV reveals further Voynichese characters that are illegible in visible light.

  — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/); [The Art Newspaper, 25 Sep 2024](https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/09/25/voynich-manuscript-scans-reveal-early-decoding-attempt)

### Inferences
- **Weakened:** the f1r "key" as an authorial cipher table, since it is a 17th-century reader's attempt. The month names also cannot be read as the author's own language. They show only that a 15th-century reader, likely in a French/Occitan or Alpine-Romance milieu, used the zodiac calendrically.
- **Constrains:** the f116v hand, Germanic and close in date to the main text, places at least one early user in a German-speaking milieu. It has been read as partly German. The often-quoted "michiton oladabas" and "so nim geismi[l]ch" readings were not confirmed in sources I opened, and the 2024 MSI changes some letter readings. Theories that build a key from the old f116v readings, for example treating "michiton oladabas" as a plaintext/ciphertext pair, rest on uncertain transcriptions.
- Romance month names in one hand and Germanic marginalia in another suggest the book moved between or sat at a linguistic border (Alpine region, Upper Rhine or southern Germany) in the 15th century. This is conjecture.

### Gaps
- The specific reading "michiton oladabas" and its proposed German or Latin glosses were not verified in a source opened this session (unverified).
- No peer-reviewed dialectological study of the month names was found.
- I did not confirm, against a primary analysis, whether the month-name ink differs chemically from the main text.

## Q6. Multispectral imaging (2014 capture, 2024 release) and other hidden-text claims

### Takeaway
**[2024]** The only published MSI covers ten leaves imaged in 2014 and released by Davis in September 2024. It recovered the Tepenec ex libris, the Marci-attributed alphabet columns on f1r and some faded marginalia. It found no palimpsest or hidden underwriting, and no key. Davis has reportedly stated that full-manuscript MSI is unlikely soon.

### Cited Findings
- **[2024]** Imaged leaves: 1r, 8r, 17r, 26r, 47r, 70v1, 71r, 93r, 102v1 (101v2) and 116v. They were imaged by the Lazarus Project in 2014 during another Yale project and never posted at the time. Partial results appeared in Clemens (ed.), *The Voynich Manuscript* (2016 Yale facsimile), pp. 31-32. Roger Easton produced new processings in September 2024. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- **[2024]** Results by leaf:
  - f26r: an offset from f25v, show-through from f26v (copper-rich green leaching), and an unexplained semicircular feature.
  - f70v1: the pale blue "text" is show-through from f70r1.
  - f71r: the turquoise and green pigments react very differently under MSI, and Davis suggests XRF.
  - f93r: text is written over a pigment stain.
  - f101v2/102v1: a drawing under a rust stain, obscured text on a blue vessel, and faded text on a fold.
  - f8r and f47r: nothing notable.
  - No palimpsest was found.

  — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- Hackaday cautions that apparent erased text can be show-through, and that MSI yielded no decryption key. — [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/blog/page/908/?cat=PHL)
- **[2025]** A Voynich Ninja post attributed to Davis (December 2025) says full MSI of the manuscript is unlikely soon, and that the 2014 images did not reveal the hidden text some hoped for. — [Voynich Ninja thread 5141](https://www.voynich.ninja/printthread.php?tid=5141) (forum, secondary)

### Inferences
- **Weakened:** theories that rely on hidden or erased underlying text, such as steganographic underwriting or a palimpsest key, at least for the ten imaged leaves.
- The f1r evidence supports the provenance chain (Horčický, then Marci) and argues against a modern forgery.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 new imaging campaign was found. The remaining roughly 230 pages have not been imaged.

## Q7. What the illustrations suggest about origin (zodiac, castle merlons, baths, plants)

### Takeaway
The iconographic evidence is suggestive, not conclusive. The commonly cited pointers are the swallowtail ("Ghibelline") merlons on the Rosettes castle, the zodiac design and month names, and bathing-scene parallels to the Italian *De balneis Puteolanis* tradition. Together they point loosely to a southern German, Alpine or northern Italian zone in the early 15th century. Almost all of this comes from blogs and forums rather than peer-reviewed art history. Plant identifications are unreliable in every direction, including the New World identifications.

### Cited Findings
- The 2009 ORF documentary suggested a northern Italian origin from the swallowtail merlons on the Rosettes castle. Critics note this is "a single detail on a single page, that might just as well be a copy." Such merlons also occur in Sicily, in 14th-century Russia, on the Kremlin (built by Italian architects around 1480), and in German, Swiss and Austrian territories. — [Cipher Mysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/category/places-of-interest/milan/page/2); [Voynich Ninja thread 92](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-92.html) (blog/forum)
- Merlons may carry a Ghibelline (pro-imperial) political meaning. Alternatively, "V" merlons were simply easier to draw at small scale. Stylistic comparisons by one poster point toward Alsace or the Lake Constance region. — [Voynich Ninja thread 92](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-92.html) (forum)
- The balneological section runs roughly f75r-f84v. Peter of Eboli's *De balneis Puteolanis* (about 1220), describing the baths near Naples, survives in about 20 illustrated copies of the 13th-15th centuries. Adam McLean compared the Voynich water section to "Balneum Sulphatara" in Valencia MS 860 (Kauffmann 1959, pl. 62). Layout and architecture are similar, but the drawing style differs. A "Rosettes = Pozzuoli map" claim is a speculative blog hypothesis. — [alchemywebsite, Voynich baths](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/Voynich_baths_01.html); [Cipher Mysteries, De balneis](https://ciphermysteries.com/tag/de-balneis)
- I found no source on a Bad Baden (Baden-Baden or Baden near Zürich or Vienna) bath theory in the results I obtained.
- Plants: Hugh O'Neill (1944) claimed a sunflower and a capsicum. Tucker & Talbert (2013) claimed 37 plants possibly of Central American origin and proposed Nahuatl; Tucker & Janick (book) expanded the claim to 169 "phytomorphs." Criticism is mostly informal, and no formal published botanical rebuttal was found. — [Fine Books Magazine, Feb 2014](https://www.finebooksmagazine.com/fine-books-news/possible-mexican-origin-voynich-manuscript); [Cipher Mysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/2014/01/21/brand-new-new-world-nahuatl-voynich-manuscript-theory)
- The painting quality is low, and some (Touwaide, privately) doubt that the original composers painted it. The colours may mislead plant identification. — [voynich.nu origin page](https://voynich.nu/extra/sp_origin.html)

### Inferences
- **Effectively ruled out on combined evidence:** New World plant identifications, given parchment of about 1404-1438 and a European ownership chain from 1599 or 1608. Tucker et al. need either a post-1520 date, which strains the C-14 and McCrone results, or a pre-Columbian contact scenario.
- **Mildly supported:** a Central European/Alpine or northern Italian origin, from the merlons, the bath iconography and the Germanic and Romance annotations. No single image is diagnostic.
- Because images appear to have been drawn before the text and painted crudely, perhaps by someone else, iconographic "readings" used as cribs, such as matching plant names to labels, rest on uncertain identifications.

### Gaps
- I found no dated, peer-reviewed costume or hairstyle analysis (for example, dating nymph headgear or the crossbow-Sagittarius detail) in the sources I opened. These are often cited in the literature but were not verified here.
- The Beinecke's own catalogue description, the essays in the 2016 Yale facsimile and the Bad Baden bath theory were not consulted directly this session (unverified).
