# Open files still leave Voynichese unread

*Synthesised 2026-10-18 21:16:51 -07:00 (local clock). Inputs: the five notes in `research_notes/Voynich public source gaps/`, checked the same evening. A source those notes mark as unopened stays unopened here.*

This file is a research note, not the project's queue or source of truth. The source of truth remains workflow/FINDINGS-GRAPH.yaml, the registers under .inventory/registers/, and the result files.

The source-of-truth map already scores the main transcriptions, Lindemann and Bowern's entropy corpus, a Naibbe-like generator, and the Timm-Schinner jar, and it already freezes a joint profile: character h2 about **2.11**, about **7,025** types, hapax about **0.68**, line-edge habits, and in-line reuse. What is still missing from that map, and is actually on the public internet, is a small set of primary documents and 2026 data files: Greg Hodgins's letter of **17 February 2009**, the McCrone letter of **1 April 2009**, Layfield and Davis's 2026 bifolio table, Zattera's Slot IVTFF, and the Chang, Koshy, Signorino, and Amador deposits. Those files tighten when the skins died, which leaves share a sheet, and which published numbers are even comparable to the frozen profile. They do not read Beinecke MS 408. Plant names and cipher proposals stay candidates. The parchment date, the hand on f1r, and the wording used for f116v are published disagreements, not one settled fact. Filing every open item below would still leave the text without a typed margin, a second herbal with per-folio names, a period codebook or combinatory grid long enough to score, a date for the ink, and any public table that hits the frozen profile jointly. The manuscript is not solved, and these sources are not sufficient to solve it.

## Hodgins dates four skins, and the catalogues have not caught up

**Radiocarbon.** The local map already uses a parchment window of 1404-1438 and already knows the test is not a place. It does not file the laboratory letter page by page, and the Yale catalogue text sitting with the images was not revised to that letter. The public primary source is Greg Hodgins, NSF-Arizona AMS Laboratory, to Christine McCarthy, **17 February 2009**, a 10-page PDF with **no licence statement**, hosted by René Zandbergen, who says it was released at Rich Santacoloma's request and that he knows of no journal article ([Hodgins letter](https://www.voynich.nu/papers/Carbon_GH_2009.pdf); [Zandbergen, carbon](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)). Four solvent-extracted samples, taken **15 January 2009**, are f8 / AA84050 **490 ± 37 BP**, f26 / AA84049 **514 ± 35 BP**, f47 / AA84048 **506 ± 35 BP**, and f68 / AA84047 **550 ± 35 BP**. The weighted mean is **516 ± 18 BP**. IntCal04 and OxCal 3.10 put that mean at **1404-1438 AD (95.4%)**. Each leaf's own 95.4% range is wider and two-peaked. f68 alone runs **1308-1362 (42.5%)** and **1385-1436 (52.9%)**, so that one skin does not exclude the late fourteenth century. Zandbergen's IntCal13 recalculation of the same fractions prints **1404-1435**, and he also prints uncalibrated **489 ± 38 BP** for f8 against Hodgins's stated **490 ± 37**. The same page gives uncalibrated central years before 1950 of 1461, 1436, 1444, and 1400. Those years are not a second calibrated date. **Clue:** "1404-1438" is the combined mean, not a writing date and not every leaf. A story that needs the skins made after 1438, or before 1404, sits in the **4.6%** tail of the mean. The writing can be later than 1438. Nothing in the letter dates the ink. Do not mix his 1435 ceiling with Hodgins's 1438 ceiling.

The catalogues disagree with the letter and with each other, in their own words. The IIIF catalog still says **"[ca. 1401-1599?]"** ([catalog 2002046](https://collections.library.yale.edu/catalog/2002046)). The older pre-1600 page still says **"Central Europe [?], s. XV ex-XVI [?]"** and still says New World plants would put the writing after 1493 ([pre1600.ms408.htm](https://pre1600ms.beinecke.library.yale.edu/docs/pre1600.ms408.htm)). The current Beinecke highlight still says the book **"was produced in Central Europe at the end of the fifteenth or during the sixteenth century"** and, separately, that **"scientific testing suggests material consistency with manuscripts produced in the fifteenth century"** ([Beinecke collection page](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript)). Lisa Fagin Davis's post of **8 September 2024** glosses a Google Drive file as **"ca. 1425"**; that Drive file was not opened, so the gloss is unverified as the Hodgins letter ([Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)). Jules Janick and Arthur O. Tucker, *Unraveling the Voynich Codex* (Springer, 2018), were not opened. Wikipedia, not the book, says they place composition **"in Mexico between 1562 and 1572"** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript)). That date is a candidate, and it conflicts with the Hodgins mean unless the book was written on old blank parchment. That escape was not checked, because the book was not opened. What would refute "no journal article reprints the lab sheets" is a 2010-2026 paper that does. None was found on **2026-10-18**.

**Pigments.** The local map's six Yale JPEGs do not include a compositional report, and the sentence "no modern components" is not in the PDF that was read. The public file is McCrone Associates project MA47613, Joseph G. Barabe to Kevin Repp, **1 April 2009**, fetched from a Yale URL whose path is dated 2026-06 ([voynich_analysis.pdf](https://library.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/voynich_analysis.pdf)). It is not a Creative Commons release. The footer says results apply only to the samples tested and that **"dissemination, interpretation, and/or reproduction, except in whole, are not recommended."** Spectra named as Figures 1A-20F are not in the file. Text ink and drawing ink are both iron-gall and, in the letter's words, **"most likely created contemporaneously."** The page-number ink, the quire-number ink, and the Latin **"a"** on f1r differ from the text ink and from each other. Blue is azurite. Green is a copper-stained organic **"optically consistent with copper resinate,"** plus a copper-chloride **"consistent with atacamite or similar,"** and infrared found no classical resin. Red-brown is red ochre. The f116v text sample is iron-gall with gum. Davis calls these tests **"XRF"** and says they **"did not find anything suspicious"** and that the pigments **"were consistent with medieval recipes."** That gloss is not in the McCrone conclusions. The methods were microscopy, infrared, X-ray diffraction, and EDS, not a standalone XRF survey. **Clue:** the sampled leaves are f26r, f47r, f78r, f86v, f116v, f70v, the f8v quire mark, and the f1r **"a"**. f71r was not tested. Titanium appears on f70v and f1r in the table and is not identified as a modern pigment in the letter text that was read. Treating it as proof of modern paint goes beyond the report. A 2014 York protein paper that Zandbergen cites for calfskin was not opened ([origin.html](https://www.voynich.nu/origin.html)). No post-2009 elemental paper was found. What would refute "McCrone is still the only compositional report in hand" is a published XRF or Raman campaign. Not found this session.

The old pre-1600 formula is the one to retire.

Their singulion sequences do not change those sheets. They are LSA permutations, and the authors say the order is unprovable until the text can be read. Torsten Timm, October 2026, accepts higher within-bifolium similarity and rejects the claim that this recovers reading order ([lingbuzz/010143](https://lingbuzz.net/lingbuzz/010143)). Paint crossing non-facing conjoints, including the streams from f78v to f81r, is their evidence that decoration preceded the nesting. Offsets show the present order is centuries old. Paula Zyats is quoted, not opened, for one late-Gothic sewing, about 1400-1500, and one earlier set of wooden covers, likely fifteenth century, before a limp vellum cover, likely nineteenth century. The Yale Press essay that contains her report is not free. Zandbergen's diagram PDF index returned Mod_Security "Not Acceptable," and whether `curr_tabA.pdf` is still up was not checked, so a conflict between that PDF and Table 5 was not tested ([descr.html](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)). **Clue:** if the misbinding is real, it is the original sewing, not a later rebinding. The proposed reading order is a labeled hypothesis. Zandbergen's production notes, which the map can already use, add no pricking, text that often avoids the drawings, and folio and quire numbers added after the mix-up and before the losses. He dates the quire-number style to the fifteenth century. That is his inference, not a lab report. Copyright on his explanatory pages is his, 2004-2026, not a reuse licence.

**Hands.** The local map has a five-scribe result and does not have a free folio-by-folio table from the paper that first published it. The 2026 HTML restates five hands, citing Davis 2020 and 2022. With two bifolio exceptions, **57|66** and **85|86**, each extant bifolio is one scribe, and f115r carries scribes 2 and 3 on one page. Table 1, which excludes f116v, ties scribe and Currier language to the section: botanical leaves use scribes 1, 2, 3, and 5 and both A and B; the cosmological leaves are scribe 4 and B; the biological leaves are scribe 2 and B; the rose sheet is scribes 2 and 4; the stars are scribes 3 and 2 and B. Herbal bifolia of different scribes are nested in the same quires. **Clue:** scribe identity follows the bifolio, not the quire. A hand test that ignores 57|66, 85|86, and f115r will not match her map. The 2020 article, *Manuscript Studies* 5.1, was located and not opened. An archive.org PDF is listed and its licence is unverified ([archive.org details](https://archive.org/details/digital-paleography-and-the-voynich-manuscript)). The 2022 title was not in the portion of the 2026 article that was returned. What would refute "no 2022 revision was retrieved" is that bibliography entry. It was not read.

## Marginal hands disagree, and none of them is typed

**f1r.** The local map can already say that a Roman alphabet column has been attributed to Marci. It cannot file a glyph-to-letter string, because nobody published one. Zandbergen's table calls the three vertical sequences **"f1r SEQ V+L"** and **"Tentatively identified as the hand of Johannes Marcus Marci,"** **"presumably a decryption attempt by a later owner"** ([writing.html](https://www.voynich.nu/writing.html)). Davis, **8 September 2024**, an author's blog with no Creative Commons line, says she compared Rauwolf, Rudolf, Sinapius, Baresch, Marci, Kircher, Dee, and Kelley, and that **"One of these men is a very good match: Johannes Marcus Marci (1595-1667)."** She dates his ownership from Baresch's death in 1662 to the 1665 gift, and she compares the hand with his autograph of **12 September 1640**, APUG 557, fol. 127r. She also writes **"Are these alphabets an early attempt to decode the manuscript? Perhaps."** The physical description is two Roman alphabets in humanistic bookhand, the second **"offset by one letter,"** plus a Voynichese series. The **"preliminary transcription"** is an image, [screenshot-696.png](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/screenshot-696.png), not a text string. Some column-2 glyphs **"cannot be clearly discerned."** **Clue:** "tentatively" and "a very good match" are the published strengths of the attribution. Neither is a signature. Two alphabets are not one clean key. A cipher test still requires a person to read that image. Forum dissent was not re-read.

**f116v and f17r.** The wording is a three-way disagreement, and one familiar label is not in the sources. Davis: **"The writing is similar to, but isn't quite, Voynichese. It has Germanic features and looks roughly contemporary with the manuscript, although it might be a bit later."** The upper margin of f17r is **"likely written by the same hand."** **"No one really knows what these inscriptions represent, or when they were written, or where, or why."** The same post does not contain the phrase **"pseudo-Germanic"** and does not give a line to copy. It does say that the third and fourth words, sometimes thought to begin with **p**, look in the MSI image as if they begin with **u** or **v**, and that faint strokes above them are stains, not ink. Show-through from f116r is mirror-writing in brown. Zandbergen's table row is a different wording: **"Recipe or spell, in a mixture of pseudo-Latin and German, with two words in Voynich script."** He prints no normalized string for f17r. Nick Pelling, **22 January 2026**, says he can read **"the f17r marginalia"** and that **"the first word actually seemed to have originally been 'meilhor'."** He frames the line as Occitan ([Pelling 2026](https://ciphermysteries.com/2026/01/22/finally-i-can-read-the-voynich-manuscript)). The words "Mallier," "aller," "balsamina," and "otaiol" were not in the slices extracted, so they are not quoted. His full line, and notes of **5 April 2026**, were not extracted. **Clue:** Davis, the palaeographer in this set, publishes the hand and a Germanic character, not a transcription. Zandbergen publishes "pseudo-Latin and German," not "pseudo-Germanic." Pelling's "meilhor" is a candidate reading of one f17r word, not an agreed edition of f116v. There is still no typed, agreed transcription of any of the three pages.

The same 2024 post is specific about the ten imaged leaves, **1r, 8r, 17r, 26r, 47r, 70v1, 71r, 93r, 102v1, and 116v**, and it is not a transcription. No palimpsest underwriting on those ten images. On f93r the text is written over the flower stain, so on that page color preceded writing. The f1r chemical stain is the one Voynich made around 1914, and MSI confirms **"Jacobi à Tepenecz."** f70v1's pale blue "text" is show-through, not hidden text. The raw stacks sit in a Google Drive folder that was not opened, so her "few other pages" remain unresolved ([Drive folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1mNQGKQDSCR4M_c2M2JrsU5soghvYwMig)). No 2025 or 2026 page that prints the f1r columns and the f17r and f116v lines as text was found outside voynich.ninja, which was out of scope. What would fill that gap is a blog post or paper that does print them. Not found.

**Whose book, and what the letters actually say.** The local notes already carry most of the letter chain. The open edition is Philip Neal's, with no licence line on the footer, beside Zandbergen's pages, which are copyright 2004-2026 and not a reuse licence. Neal's dateline is **"Prague 19 August 1665."** The letter says the book was left by an unnamed friend, and that **"Doctor Raphael"** said it had belonged to Rudolf, who **"presented 600 ducats to the messenger who brought him the book,"** and thought the author was Roger Bacon. Marci suspends judgment ([Neal 1665 English](http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/marci_1665_english/)). The letter does not name Mnishovsky, a seller, or a purchase year. "Raphael Mnishovsky" is an identification, not a name in the letter. The Beinecke overview still says Marci sent the book **"in 1666"** and that Voynich bought it in 1912 **"from the Jesuit College at Frascati."** Neal's notes on whether the year-line has been disputed were not opened, so the conflict on the page is between his translation and the library prose. Kinner's letters of **4 January 1666** and **5 January 1667** are Zandbergen's evidence that the book was sent; the 1667 letter names the carrier as Fr. Daniel Krupsky in 1665. Those two English pages of Neal's were not opened. The earliest secure document is Kircher's reply of **12 March 1639**, which presupposes a lost approach of late 1637. Baresch, **21 April 1639**, writes that **"it is my guess that the whole thing is medical."** That is a reader's hypothesis, not a decipherment. His death is another disagreement: Zandbergen says **"between 1646 and 1662,"** Davis writes **"Baresch's death in 1662,"** and the Beinecke overview prints **"Georgius Barschius (1585-1622),"** which cannot survive the 1639 letter. The enclosed decipherment notes Marci says he sent **"have not been preserved."**

The carbon result makes a book manufactured for Rudolf on new parchment very hard, and it leaves a later purchase of an old book untouched. Dee as the seller is retired in Zandbergen's summary of Rafal Prinke (2011, 2016). Those Prinke papers were not opened, so the three failed props are his summary, not a fresh reading of Prinke. The **600 ducats** remain one sentence of hearsay plus Zandbergen's working assumption, quoted: **"I am assuming that Rudolf II owned the MS, and that, indeed, it was sold to him by Karl Widemann."** The nearest account he cites is March 1599, **500 Taler**, **"close to 600 gold florins,"** and it does not name this manuscript. He also writes that 600 ducats **"is entirely possible"** as the price of a set, not of this book alone ([history.html](https://www.voynich.nu/history.html); [sp_history](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/sp_history.html)). Stefan Guzy's 2022 conference PDF is open and was not opened, so his exact claim is not quoted from him ([CEUR paper 16](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/paper16.pdf)). The 2023 Herold-Jahrbuch follow-up is not open. The f1r ex libris of Jacobus de Tepenec, a Rudolf courtier in **1608-1611**, is the Prague-court link that does not rest on Marci. Zandbergen puts the rescued Collegium Romanum books at Villa Torlonia in Castel Gandolfo, against the Frascati sentence. **Clue:** defended, and still not a reading, is a Rudolf-circle connection after 1608. Dropped, on Zandbergen's summary, is Dee in 1586. Not proved is that Rudolf paid 600 ducats for this codex. The zodiac glosses are a separate later hand, not Voynichese: `mars`, `aberil`, `may`, `jong`, `iollet`, `augst`, `septe(m)b(r)`, `octe(m)bre`, `nove(m)bre`, `decebre`. He argues Northern French from `jong` and sets Spanish and Occitan aside. A note of **27 March 2026** on his site does not revise them ([notice.html](https://www.voynich.nu/notice.html)). No study consulted reports a Voynichese word unique to one month. Pelling's Occitan page on the month names was not re-fetched. Capricorn and Aquarius are not in the table.

## Conclusion

The public remainder is real, and it is almost entirely a control shelf. The files worth fetching measure skins, inks, sheets, scribe-to-bifolium attachment, transcription disagreement, and a set of 2026 statistics that use a different segmentation or a different entropy than the frozen profile. Importing Chang's top-1 accuracy, Signorino's 3.49 bits, Parisel's end-class rate, or Greshko's rowwise 1.954 as if they confirmed or refuted h2 about 2.11, 7,025 types, and hapax about 0.68 would be a category error. The same error is available in the wording fights. A catalogue sentence, a blog gloss, and a primary letter are three different dates. "A very good match" and "tentatively" are two different strengths of the f1r hand. "Germanic features," "pseudo-Latin and German," and Pelling's "meilhor" are three different claims about the margins, and "pseudo-Germanic" is none of them.

After every open item below was filed, the manuscript would still be unread, and the sources would still be insufficient to read it. What would still be missing is a typed, agreed text of f1r, f17r, and f116v; a date for the ink as distinct from the death of the animals; elemental results for surfaces McCrone did not sample, f71r among them, and the spectra that are not in the public PDF; a second herbal in the same tradition as Canon. Misc. 408 with its own folio names; an open Tranchedino transcription or any combinatory grid longer than the short sample already scored; a diplomatic shorthand corpus longer than the abbreviation controls already rejected for h2; and one public table that reports the frozen five-part profile for any generator. Layfield and Davis already say their reading order is unprovable until the text can be read. The lost 1637 approach and the decipherment notes Marci says he enclosed have not survived, so no download will produce them. None of the open files is that missing text, and none of the candidates is a solution.
