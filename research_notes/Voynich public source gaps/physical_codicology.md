# Physical and codicological evidence for Beinecke MS 408

Fetched 2026-10-18 (local clock 21:05 -07:00). Physical claims only. Decipherment and LSA reading-order scores are marked as hypotheses, not facts. Already held locally and not repeated: Zandbergen-Landini EVA, six Yale JPEGs, word boxes, and the note that the 2014/2024 raw stacks cover about ten leaves. voynich.ninja was not used.

## What is the current published collation, and which folio pairs are claimed as conjoint?

### Takeaway
The free 2026 collation is eighteen extant quires (sixteen and eighteen lost), 102 leaves, and the bifolio pairs in Layfield and Davis Table 5. They argue the fifteenth-century sewing itself nested single bifolia out of an intended singulion order. That reordering is a textual hypothesis. It does not change which leaves share a sheet.

## What did the Arizona radiocarbon tests date, and where is the primary report?

### Takeaway
The primary report is Greg Hodgins's letter of 17 February 2009, not a journal article. Four parchment samples, taken 15 January 2009, give a weighted mean of 516 ± 18 BP. IntCal04 and OxCal 3.10 put that mean at 1404-1438 AD (95.4%). Each leaf's own 95.4% range is wider and two-peaked. The test dates the animal's death, not the writing.

### Cited Findings
- Letter: Greg Hodgins, NSF-Arizona AMS Laboratory, to Christine McCarthy, Yale, 17 February 2009. Samples from leaves 8, 26, 47, and 68. Lab numbers AA84050, AA84049, AA84048, and AA84047 respectively. Public PDF, 10 pages, no licence statement. Zandbergen says it was released at Rich Santacoloma's request and that he knows of no published paper. Host date on his page: latest update 11/06/2025; copyright René Zandbergen for the explanatory page, not stated for the letter. — [Carbon_GH_2009.pdf](https://www.voynich.nu/papers/Carbon_GH_2009.pdf); [carbon.html](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- Uncalibrated ages, solvent-extracted, isotope-fractionation corrected (letter Tables 1-2 and Figures 1-4). Calibration IntCal04 (Reimer et al. 2004), OxCal v3.10:
  - f8 / AA84050: left edge, 12-17 cm from top; 7.84 mg dry; δ13C -22.6‰; fraction modern 0.9409 ± 0.0044; 490 ± 37 BP. 68.2%: 1414-1442 AD. 95.4%: 1327-1342 (3.2%) and 1394-1458 (92.2%).
  - f26 / AA84049: right edge, 3.5-6.7 cm and 15.3-19.5 cm from top; 5.88 mg; δ13C -23.3‰; 0.9380 ± 0.0041; 514 ± 35 BP. 68.2%: 1404-1438 AD. 95.4%: 1321-1349 (12.0%) and 1391-1447 (83.4%).
  - f47 / AA84048: left edge, 14-18.7 cm from top; 5.60 mg; δ13C -23.0‰; 0.9389 ± 0.0041; 506 ± 35 BP. 68.2%: 1409-1439 AD. 95.4%: 1324-1345 (7.2%) and 1392-1449 (88.2%).
  - f68 / AA84047: left edge, 0-2.5 cm from bottom; 10.82 mg; δ13C -22.4‰; 0.9338 ± 0.0041; 550 ± 35 BP. 68.2%: 1324-1345 (26.1%) and 1393-1422 (42.1%). 95.4%: 1308-1362 (42.5%) and 1385-1436 (52.9%).
  - Same f68 sample without solvent extraction: 488 ± 35 BP; δ13C -21.4‰; 95.4% mostly 1396-1457. Hodgins excluded it. He says the unextracted sample looked younger, consistent with recent carbon from handling.
- Weighted mean of the four extracted measurements: 516 ± 18 BP. 68.2%: 1411-1430 AD. 95.4%: 1404-1438 AD (Figure 7). Clue: the quoted "1404-1438" is this mean, not a single leaf. Leaf 26's 68.2% range is also 1404-1438, which is a different interval. f68 alone does not exclude a late fourteenth-century skin.
- Why these leaves (Zandbergen, not the letter): f8 is the other half of the f1 bifolio (Tepenec ex libris; f1 too stained); f26 herbal B, thin, also sampled for chemistry; f47 herbal A, thick bifolio 42+47; f68 an astronomical foldout. Press conference 8 December 2009, for an ORF documentary. — [carbon.html](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- Zandbergen's own IntCal13 recalculation, same fractions, gives a combined 95% interval of 1404-1435. He says the upper end differs from Hodgins because of interpolation, and that the curves are not visually distinguishable. He also prints uncalibrated 489 ± 38 BP for f8 against Hodgins's stated 490 ± 37. Clue: do not mix his 1435 ceiling with Hodgins's 1438 ceiling. — [carbon.html](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)

### Inferences
- A theory that needs the skins made after 1438 has to sit in the 4.6% tail of the mean, or reject the letter. A theory that needs them made before 1404 is in the same tail. Neither tail is zero.
- The writing can be later than 1438. Nothing in the letter dates the ink.

### Gaps
- No journal article was found. What would refute that: a 2010-2026 paper that reprints the lab sheets. Not found this session.
- Davis's 2024 post links a Drive file as the carbon report and glosses the date as "ca. 1425." That Drive file was not opened, so it is unverified as the same letter. — [Davis, 8 Sep 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)

## What do McCrone and later pigment studies say is on the parchment?

### Takeaway
The report in hand is McCrone Associates project MA47613, letter of 1 April 2009, signed by Joseph G. Barabe. Text and drawing inks are iron-gall with gum. Blue is azurite. Green is a copper organic with a copper-chloride phase. Red-brown is red ochre. The public PDF does not say "no modern components." No later elemental report was opened.

### Cited Findings
- Letter to Kevin Repp, Beinecke. Examined and sampled at Sterling Library conservation lab, 14-15 January 2009, for ProOmnia Film and Video. Methods: polarized-light microscopy, infrared spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometry in the SEM. Not a standalone XRF report. Codex described as 23.5 cm high, 16.2 cm wide, about 5 cm deep, about 240 pages, fair condition, vellum pages, vellum binding. UV: writing is deep velvety purple-black, "suggesting an iron gall ink." f1r under UV showed a signature "probably that of Jacobi à Tepenec," other undeciphered writing, and blotchiness "consistent with chemical staining" that may have been applied to enhance writing and may have washed some of it away. Public file read this session: [library.yale.edu PDF, path dated 2026-06](https://library.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/voynich_analysis.pdf), linked from the [Beinecke collection page](https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/beinecke/collections/beinecke-cipher-voynich-manuscript). Footer: results apply only to the samples tested; "dissemination, interpretation, and/or reproduction, except in whole, are not recommended." Not a Creative Commons release. The 8-page file is the 6-page letter plus Table I (2 pages). Spectra and photomicrographs named as Figures 1A-20F are not in this file.
- Conclusions, quoted in substance from pp. 5-6: text ink and drawing ink are both iron-gall and "most likely created contemporaneously" (p. 3: one case of paint over writing, Figure 7A, figure not in the PDF). Page-number ink, quire-number ink, and the Latin "a" on f1r differ from the text ink and from each other. Blue is ground azurite. Green is copper-stained amorphous organic material "optically consistent with copper resinate," plus copper-chloride "consistent with atacamite or similar," bulk non-crystalline, no classical resin found by IR. Binder of the inks and the green is gum. Sample 20's binder is undetermined. Blue and red-brown were not IR-tested; gum called "likely."
- Table I locations and IDs (the clue is which folio was actually sampled):
  - 1-5, all f26r: text iron-gall (3.5 cm from top, 10.2 cm from right); drawing ink iron-gall plus potassium lead oxide, potassium hydrogen phosphate, syngenite (7.3 cm / 5.5 cm); green leaf, copper-organic, atacamite possible, calcium sulfate and carbonate (14.2 / 6.7); blue flower, azurite and minor cuprite (2.3 / 4.0); red-brown root, red ochre (hematite), lead oxide, potassium compounds (18.7 / 9.1).
  - 6-8, f47r: text iron-gall, low iron (8.4 cm from top, 12.6 from right); green leaf, copper-organic, atacamite probable (4.3 / 5.4); red-brown root, hematite, iron sulfide, palmierite (0.8 cm from bottom, 8.8 from right).
  - 9-12, f78r: text iron-gall with mercury traces (3.6 from top, 12.7 from right); blue water, azurite and minor cuprite (7.4 / 2.8); green pool, copper-organic, atacamite probable, tin and iron compounds, azurite and cuprite traces (3.6 from bottom, 4.8 from right); clear material from a bather's headdress, calcium carbonate and protein "possibly eggwhite" (3.6 from bottom, 4.9 from right).
  - 13-14, f86v: text iron-gall (2.5 from top, 6.7 from left); drawing ink iron-gall, low iron (4.0 from top, 1.0 from right).
  - 15, f26r page number "26": iron-gall, high phosphorus and iron (0.8 / 0.8 from top and right).
  - 16, f116v text: iron-gall, high iron, gum (6.9 from top, 2.9 from left). No photograph.
  - 17-18, f70v, woman's face, 12.5 cm from top, 14.3 from left: drawing ink iron-gall, mercury, a titanium-compound particle, a tin compound; clear-white paint proteinaceous with starch traces.
  - 19, f8v quire mark: iron-gall, very low iron (0.2 cm from bottom, 12.8 from left).
  - 20, f1r, black ink of an "a" under a chemical stain, 2.5 cm from top, 0.6 from right: iron-gall, very low iron, bone black, a titanium compound, binder undetermined. Prose says bone black and mineral particles, suspects protein from the parchment, and says a proteinaceous binder is unlikely. It does not explain the titanium row.
- Davis, 8 Sep 2024, calls these "XRF" tests and says they "did not find anything suspicious" and that pigments "were consistent with medieval recipes." That gloss is not in the McCrone conclusions. She also says f71r was not tested, and that its turquoise and green behave differently under MSI, so XRF of f71r would be new. Her McCrone link is `https://beinecke.library.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/voynich_analysis.pdf`. A HEAD on that URL returned `text/html`, not a PDF. The file actually read is the `library.yale.edu` path above. — [Davis 2024](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/2024/09/08/multispectral-imaging-and-the-voynich-manuscript/)
- Parchment species, separate from McCrone: Zandbergen says a 2014 York protein analysis found calfskin, so vellum in the strict sense, and a parchment maker in November 2014 called the cover goat. Primary York paper not opened. — [origin.html](https://www.voynich.nu/origin.html)

### Inferences
- The page number, the quire mark, and the f1r "a" are later or at least different campaigns from the main text, on McCrone's own comparison. That does not date them.
- Titanium on f70v and f1r is in the table. It is not identified as a modern pigment in the letter text that was read. Treating it as proof of modern paint would go beyond the report.

### Gaps
- No post-2009 elemental reanalysis was opened. What would refute "McCrone is still the only compositional report": a published XRF or Raman campaign. Not found this session.
- Figures 1A-20F are not in the public PDF. Sample 2's lead-bearing crystals, which EDS did not confirm as lead, are flagged by McCrone as needing more work.

Both claims conflict with the radiocarbon letter and with later Beinecke pages that drop Dee. No offset list. — [pre1600.ms408.htm](https://pre1600ms.beinecke.library.yale.edu/docs/pre1600.ms408.htm)
- Condition and offsets not in the catalogue: McCrone, "fair condition," chemical blotches on f1r (1 April 2009). Zandbergen: average parchment, some holes and production stitches, flesh and hair sides hard to tell, insect holes and edge discoloration on the first and last leaves taken as evidence of earlier wooden boards covered in tanned leather ([origin.html](https://www.voynich.nu/origin.html)). Layfield and Davis: pigment offsets and the f78v|f81r streams, one fifteenth-century sewing. Davis 2024: f26r offset of f25v visible only under MSI; f93r text over a pigment spill; no palimpsest on the ten imaged leaves.

### Inferences
- Do not cite the catalogue date "[ca. 1401-1599?]" or "s. XV ex-XVI" against the Hodgins mean. The catalogue was not revised to the 2009 letter. Three Yale texts (pre-1600 page, highlight page, digital catalog) still disagree with 1404-1438 and with each other.
- A full-page image set is public. Holding only six JPEGs is a local limit, not a Yale limit. The rights statement does not make the images CC0.
